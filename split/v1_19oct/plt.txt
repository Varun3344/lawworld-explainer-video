# 1 · REJECTED OR RETURNED?
8 | You file a civil suit. The judge says this plaint cannot proceed. But is it being rejected, or returned?
6 | Those words lead to different procedures and different remedies.
8 | Let us understand Order Seven of the Code of Civil Procedure, through four leading Supreme Court judgments. || Let us understand Order VII of the Code of Civil Procedure, through four leading Supreme Court judgments.
# 2 · THE TWO PROVISIONS
8 | Order Seven, Rule Ten deals with return of the plaint for presentation to the proper court.
8 | That commonly happens when territorial or pecuniary jurisdiction is missing.
8 | Rule Eleven deals with rejection on specified grounds. Return asks whether this is the proper court.
10 | Rejection asks whether the plaint attracts a statutory ground that stops it from proceeding. A weak case alone is not enough.
# 3 · SIX GROUNDS FOR REJECTION
6 | Rule Eleven lists six grounds. First, the plaint discloses no cause of action.
8 | Second, the relief is undervalued, and the plaintiff fails to correct it within the time fixed.
8 | Third, the plaint is insufficiently stamped, and the plaintiff fails to supply the stamp paper within time.
8 | Fourth, the suit appears from the statement in the plaint to be barred by law. Fifth, the plaint is not filed in duplicate.
8 | Sixth, the plaintiff fails to comply with Rule Nine. For valuation and stamp defects, remember the chance to correct them.
# 4 · A MEANINGFUL READING
10 | The first judgment is T Arivandandam versus T V Satyapal. The Supreme Court said the plaint must receive a meaningful reading. || The first judgment is T. Arivandandam v. T.V. Satyapal. The Supreme Court said the plaint must receive a meaningful reading.
8 | Clever drafting cannot manufacture a cause of action where none exists. The court must examine the substance.
10 | Picture a plaint full of impressive allegations, but missing the material facts that create a right to relief.
10 | But the court must identify a ground under Rule Eleven. It cannot reject a plaint merely because the case looks difficult.
# 5 · THE PLAINT AND ITS DOCUMENTS
10 | Next comes Dahiben versus Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali. Read the plaint as a whole, with the documents it relies on. || Next comes Dahiben v. Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali. Read the plaint as a whole, with the documents it relies on.
6 | The defendant's competing version does not decide a Rule Eleven application.
6 | In Dahiben, the Court upheld the rejection of a suit challenging a sale deed.
6 | The suit was barred by limitation, and lacked a sustainable cause of action.
10 | So check the pleaded dates and documents carefully. Limitation supports rejection only when the bar is apparent from the plaint.
6 | Disputed facts cannot simply be resolved against the plaintiff at this stage.
# 6 · HOW RETURN WORKS
6 | Now consider return. A properly pleaded claim may still have been filed in the wrong court.
8 | Under Rule Ten, the plaint is returned for presentation to the court where the suit should have been instituted.
8 | The judge endorses the dates of presentation and return, the presenter's name, and brief reasons.
6 | Where its conditions apply, Rule Ten A can fix an appearance date in the proper court. || Where its conditions apply, Rule 10A can fix an appearance date in the proper court.
# 7 · IN THE PROPER COURT
10 | In O N G C versus Modern Construction, the Supreme Court explained what happens when a returned plaint is presented to the competent court. || In ONGC v. Modern Construction, the Supreme Court explained what happens when a returned plaint is presented to the competent court.
10 | It is treated as a fresh institution, and the trial proceeds afresh. It does not continue the earlier suit, so limitation matters.
10 | Section Fourteen of the Limitation Act may exclude the time spent pursuing the case in a court without jurisdiction.
6 | That protection must be established; return does not preserve the original filing date.
# 8 · RETURN ≠ TRANSFER
8 | The three judge decision in E X L Careers versus Frankfinn Aviation Services confirmed this rule. || The three-judge decision in EXL Careers v. Frankfinn Aviation Services confirmed this rule.
6 | After a return under Rules Ten and Ten A, the proceedings must begin afresh. || After a return under Rules 10 and 10A, the proceedings must begin afresh.
10 | Rule Ten A simplifies appearance and service; it does not turn a return into a transfer that keeps the earlier trial stage. || Rule 10A simplifies appearance and service; it does not turn a return into a transfer that keeps the earlier trial stage.
10 | One qualification: in E X L itself, the Supreme Court used its constitutional powers on exceptional facts. || One qualification: in EXL itself, the Supreme Court used its constitutional powers on exceptional facts.
6 | It left the High Court's order undisturbed. That does not change the general rule.
# 9 · DIFFERENT REMEDIES
8 | The remedies differ too. Rejection of a plaint is deemed a decree under Section Two, sub section Two.
6 | So it ordinarily attracts a first appeal under Section Ninety Six.
10 | An order returning a plaint is appealable under Order Forty Three, Rule One, clause A, subject to the Rule Ten A exception. || An order returning a plaint is appealable under Order XLIII Rule 1(a), subject to the Rule 10A exception.
8 | And rejection does not bar a fresh plaint: Rule Thirteen expressly preserves that possibility.
10 | But a fresh plaint must still overcome limitation and the legal defect. Refiling a barred claim does not remove the bar.
# 10 · APPLY THE DISTINCTION
10 | Try two examples. A plaint pleads a recoverable debt, but the chosen court lacks territorial jurisdiction. Think return, under Rule Ten.
10 | Another plaint, read with its own documents, discloses no cause of action. Think rejection, under Rule Eleven.
10 | Before drafting or opposing either application, check the forum, the material facts, the documents relied on, and limitation.
8 | Remember: return sends the plaint to the proper court; rejection follows a specified statutory ground.
6 | Get it right, because that distinction decides the next step in the entire case.
