// Facts used: RHB's SLP against the Rajasthan High Court (Jaipur Bench) judgment of 06.07.2026 dismissing an Article 227 petition (S.B. Civil
// Writ Petition No. 4122/2026) filed by RHB with the State of Rajasthan and the Special Officer, UDH (1); the petition assailed LARRA, Jaipur's
// order of 26.05.2025 allowing the reference sought by the first respondent under Section 64 of the 2013 Act, and its order of 12.09.2025
// making a minor modification in the first respondent's favour while allowing its review petition (2); the High Court: writ not
// maintainable given the appeal under Section 74(1); even treated as an appeal, the proviso is attracted on the belated approach - no
// appeal 60 days beyond the 60-day period can be entertained; the right to maintain an appeal stood foreclosed after the maximum
// condonable period (3); RHB relied on S.V. Global Mill (2026 SCC OnLine SC 171, a coordinate Bench): the 60 days in the proviso held
// "not to be mandatory", went unnoticed by the High Court; sought liberty to approach the appellate forum with a condonation application
// (4); this Court's summary of S.V. Global Mill: Section 74 does not exclude Sections 4-24 of the Limitation Act (5); the Bench "expressed
// reservation" whether it lays down correct law; counsel then asked for a reference to a larger Bench (6); reference not warranted; the
// ratio of S.V. Global Mill does not apply here; the Bench briefly observes why the precedent "does not commend our acceptance" (7);
// SEBI, Arbitration (S.34), FEMA, PMLA, Electricity, Companies, IBC - "but not thereafter" / "not exceeding" held to bar condonation
// beyond the outer limit (8, 9); Section 74(1) is in pari materia; S.V. Global Mill "does not accord with the precedents" (10); it renders
// the proviso otiose and denudes the 120-day outer limit of meaning; the legislature impliedly excluded Section 5 (11); Hukumdev Narain
// Yadav (3-Judge) binding, distinguished in S.V. Global Mill on a "specious ground"; "reverse prejudice": the State / acquiring bodies too
// could seek condonation of inordinate delay, to the landowners' detriment (14-16); the proviso is an in-built Section 5-like provision
// with a statutory cap; no condonation beyond the second 60 days (17); "the period of 120 days in Section 74 is inflexible and rigid", no
// appeal "barred even by a single day's delay"; "Even this Court in exercise of its power under Article 142 cannot condone the delay"
// (20); a reference "is, indeed, not an available option"; S.V. Global Mill "ought not to have placed a discordant construction" and could
// itself have referred; "we decline a reference" (21); even assuming S.V. Global Mill is right, its ratio is inapplicable: RHB never
// availed the Section 74 appeal, it invoked Article 227; the High Court "was perfectly justified in spurning the challenge" (23); no reason
// to refer; "pending time-barred appeals under Section 74 of the 2013 Act may be decided by the relevant high courts in accordance with
// law" (24); SLP dismissed (25).
// NOT said (kept off speech and cards): that S.V. Global Mill is OVERRULED (a coordinate two-Judge Bench; this Bench disagreed, declined

// is a landowner (the order never says what it is: "a company"); the delay in days (not given).
