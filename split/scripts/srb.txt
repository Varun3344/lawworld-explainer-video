// Facts used: 'Agreement for Development' dated 06.08.2003, five Thanawalas (the owners, the 'Vendors') and the builder firm with its

// Taluka & District Thane (8); the owners' undivided share 20/90th = 2/9th (15); Clause 1: the right to develop their 2/9th share in
// the FSI (17); Clause 3: Rs 915 per sq ft on 25,000 sq ft of FSI = Rs 2,28,75,000, open to change with the FSI till the plan is
// sanctioned (17, 57); Clause 5: Rs 55,00,000 earnest; Clause 6: balance Rs 1,73,75,000 in ten instalments of Rs 17,37,500 (18, 19);
// Clause 7: notice + 15 days to cure, payments and "time shall be the essence" (19), again Clauses 63, 64 (29); Clause 37: the
// conveyance not "till payment of all the instalments and amounts due" (27, 58); bungalow, gardens, Room No. 1 in Chawl No. 1
// excluded (16, 20, 28, 58); Clauses 53, 54: on a payment default the agreement ends; with no construction on their share, the owners
// repay the consideration, without interest (28, 62).
// Payment: no plan was sanctioned, so the third mode applied: full payment by 01.03.2008 (54); instalments 1-6 paid 2 to 50 days late,
// accepted without protest (the missing Clause 7 notice cured that delay, but time stayed of the essence) (55, 56); cheques for
// instalments 7-9 offered 144 / 52 / 117 days late, returned 16.04.2008; the 10th never tendered; the balance "was not completed by
// 01.03.2008" (55); termination by the owners' legal notice dated 09.04.2008 (2).
// Award: majority award 10.12.2011 (Justice S.D. Pandit, umpire, and P.S. Mudholkar; V.P. Patil dissented) (2, 3): termination
// illegal; specific performance on depositing Rs 10,91,30,164 within 3 months; sale deed for the 2/9th share excluding only the owners'
// share in the bungalow, garden, room and courtyard (3, 39); six times the agreed rate = Rs 5,490 per sq ft, Rs 13,72,75,000 in all
// (38); alternative damages Rs 23,21,19,892 if specific performance were reversed (38).
// Courts below: Principal District Judge, Thane, 29.03.2017, remitted by consent by the Bombay High Court on 26.06.2019 (5); second
// round 25.01.2020: both s.34 applications dismissed, the majority award upheld "in its entirety" (6); Bombay High Court (a learned
// Judge) 05.03.2024: the owners' s.37 appeal allowed, the majority award set aside (6), contrary to s.28(3), no rewriting (41).
// Held: s.37 jurisdiction coterminous with s.34 (Gayatri Balasamy) (44); unamended s.34 applies, both s.34 applications filed long
// before 23.10.2015 (CMA 35 of 2012, CMA 76 of 2013) (5, 44); an award against the terms of the contract is patently illegal (R.S.
// Sharma, OPG Power) (47, 50); "the nomenclature of a document would not be decisive as to its nature" (14); development or sale, "it
// was not essential to identify and label the character of the agreement": the terms, "stipulating sequential performance of
// obligations", mattered (52); s.28(3): decide by the terms of the contract (52); "it was not for the two learned arbitrators to
// rewrite its terms" (58); the conveyance was linked to full payment, the consideration still indeterminate (57, 58); excluded property
// pulled in, the owners of the other 7/9th not before the tribunal (58); the superseded MoU payment (Rs 13,18,334) and Rs 26,222 beyond
// the agreement (59); the new rate "completely beyond the scope of the agreement and defied logic" (59); no damages clause, alternative
// damages "opposed to public policy, as rightly noted by the High Court" (60); the arbitrators "completely supplanted them with new
// terms, as per their own fancies" (61); the High Court's s.28(3) finding "correct", its sale-or-development exercise "not necessary"
// (61). Result: "no grounds to uphold the majority award"; Article 142: the owners did not abide by Clause 54 and retained the money,
// so they return the amounts paid under the agreement with simple interest @ 9% p.a. from each payment till the refund; the builder's
// deposits in the courts returned with interest accrued, if any; appeals disposed of; parties bear their own costs (62).
// Not said: the Court's general stamp-duty remark (14) is not tied to anyone; the builder's 2017 deposit of Rs 41,11,744 and its offer to
// pay Rs 13,72,75,000 (53) are only its statements; any refund amount (the judgment gives none); the appeals were "disposed of", not
// "dismissed".
