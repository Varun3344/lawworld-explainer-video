// Facts used (para): the petitioner is aggrieved by the respondents retaining her original certificates (SSC marks list,
// Intermediate memo, study and conduct, transfer and migration certificates) and demanding the entire course fee, Rs.5,46,978 a
// year, for their release (1, prayer p.3). NEET 2019; BDS seat in the counselling conducted by R2 (the University); she gave her
// original certificates to R2, which forwarded them to R1, the college she was allotted to (2). Medical complications in her right
// leg, an operation, post-operation complications: she could not join the course and sought the certificates back - letter of
// 17.12.2019 to R2 (copy to R1) and a legal notice of 16.03.2020 (3); R1's reply notice called on her to pay the entire fee,
// Rs.5,46,978 a year for four years (3). Petitioner: the demand is contrary to UGC guidelines; settled law that colleges cannot keep
// certificates and demand the fee for returning them (4). R1's counter: a private unaided institution with no government aid, so
// the writ petition is not maintainable; the certificates were submitted at joining on 14.09.2019; she did not pay the first-year fee
// of Rs.5,49,000, got 3 more days at her father's request, and did not join or pay (5); no objection to returning them if she pays
// the entire course fee of Rs.21,96,000 with other fees and the cancellation fee payable to R2; private colleges cannot be deprived
// of the fee for the whole year / entire course when students voluntarily drop out; no refund for a voluntary drop-out (6); G.O.Ms.
// No.72 dated 15.07.2019: course discontinuation fee Rs.1,00,000 for BDS (Rs.3,00,000 MBBS) plus GST, payable to the University (7).
// R2's Standing Counsel "fairly admits" the full-course-fee demand for returning the certificates is not permissible under the UGC
// guidelines and regulations (8). Point: whether R1 has a bona fide right to retain the certificates and demand the course fee for
// returning them (10). UGC notification, October 2018 (s.12(d) r/w 12(j) UGC Act, 1956): to curb coercive and profiteering practices
// on fee refunds and retention of original certificates (11); R1's claim is "evidently contrary" to it (12); cl. 4.2.1 no insistence
// on originals with the admission form (13); cl. 4.2.2 verify originals at admission and return them immediately (14); cl. 4.2.3
// self-attested copies valid; originals used for any later verification returned at once (15); cl. 4.2.4 "Taking the certificates
// into institutional custody under any circumstances or pretext is strictly prohibited." (16); cl. 4.2.5 no retention even on
// suspicion (17). Held: "Original academic certificates are the property of the student."; Article 21 (livelihood and education);
// "An institution has no lien over them."; "Even if the College has a legitimate claim for the Tuition Fee or any other fee,
