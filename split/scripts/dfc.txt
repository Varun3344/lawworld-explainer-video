# 1 · THE COURT SCREENS THE CASE
8 | Charge sheet filed? That does not mean a full trial. The court must first screen the case, and that screening is where a sharp advocate can win early.
6 | The real question is simple: is there enough material to proceed against your client? If not, discharge.
10 | Under the B N S S, two tools do this screening: discharge and framing of charge. Know the court, section and deadline, and you can spare your client an unnecessary trial.
# 2 · WHAT DISCHARGE MEANS
6 | What is discharge? The court finds that proceeding against the accused is not justified at this stage.
8 | It is not bail, which concerns liberty while the case continues. And it is not an acquittal after trial either.
10 | Discharge needs judicial consideration and recorded reasons. A denial alone will not get it, and neither will simply filing an application.
# 3 · SESSIONS CASES
6 | Sessions case first. Take an ordinary case committed under Section Two Thirty Two.
6 | Section Two Fifty lets the accused apply for discharge within sixty days from commitment.
10 | The Judge considers the record and documents, and hears both sides. No sufficient ground for proceeding? The Judge must discharge the accused, and record reasons.
8 | But if there is ground to presume an offence only a Sessions Court can try, Section Two Fifty One requires a written charge.
6 | That charge must be framed within sixty days from the first hearing on charge. A different starting point!
8 | Some High Courts treat the sixty day application window as directory. Even so, never gamble on it. File within time.
# 4 · MAGISTRATE WARRANT CASES
8 | Different court, different clock. In a Magistrate's warrant case on a police report, Section Two Sixty Two applies.
8 | Here the accused may apply for discharge within sixty days from the supply of document copies under Section Two Thirty.
8 | The Magistrate considers the police report and papers, and hears both sides. Charge groundless? The Magistrate must discharge, with reasons.
8 | If the test for proceeding is met, Section Two Sixty Three requires a written charge within sixty days from the first hearing on charge.
# 5 · WHICH EVENT STARTS THE CLOCK
8 | This is where advocates slip, so take three dates. A Sessions case is committed on the first of June. That starts its discharge window. || This is where advocates slip, so take three dates. A Sessions case is committed on 1 June. That starts its discharge window.
10 | In a different case, a Magistrate's warrant case on a police report, copies are supplied on the tenth of June. That starts its own application window. || In a different case, a Magistrate's warrant case on a police report, copies are supplied on 10 June. That starts its own application window.
6 | If the first hearing on charge is on the first of July, the charge framing period runs from that hearing. || If the first hearing on charge is on 1 July, the charge-framing period runs from that hearing.
8 | Record the correct trigger for each clock: not the arrest, not the F I R, and never one date for every deadline.
# 6 · WHAT A CHARGE DOES
6 | What does a charge really do? It is the court's formal statement of the offence your client must answer.
10 | Sections Two Thirty Four and Two Thirty Five cover its contents: the offence, the law, the time, the place, and the person involved. Check every particular.
10 | Under Sections Two Fifty One and Two Sixty Three, the charge is read and explained, and the accused is asked whether he pleads guilty or claims trial.
8 | If he claims trial, the case moves into evidence. A charge does not establish guilt. The prosecution must still prove its case.
# 7 · NO MINI TRIAL
8 | What test applies at this stage? Does the material supply a legally sufficient basis to proceed against your client?
10 | Here is the line: strong suspicion founded on the material can justify framing a charge. Proof beyond reasonable doubt belongs to the decision on guilt.
6 | The court must apply its mind, but it should not run a mini trial to decide which witness is truthful.
10 | Equally, a bare accusation without the required legal ingredients is not enough. So test the material against every ingredient.
6 | And the Supreme Court has repeatedly distinguished this screening from deciding guilt.
# 8 · ARJUN'S CASE
8 | Picture this: Arjun is accused of stealing Ravi's laptop, worth sixty thousand rupees. Look at two possible files.
10 | First file: the prosecution papers, even accepted at face value, contain nothing connecting Arjun to the theft. Argue that the charge is groundless, and seek discharge.
10 | Second file: a witness account and supporting material link him to it. Then a dispute about the witness's reliability may need evidence at trial.
6 | The actual record controls the result, and neither example guarantees an outcome.
# 9 · OTHER CASE TYPES
10 | One more trap: these sixty day clocks do not fit every case. Warrant cases not started on a police report follow Sections Two Sixty Seven to Two Sixty Nine.
10 | In summons cases, Section Two Seventy Four generally needs no formal charge. The particulars of the accusation are simply stated to the accused.
10 | But if the accusation is groundless, the Magistrate must record reasons and release the accused. That release then has the effect of a discharge.
6 | Special courts and special statutes may have additional rules of their own. Check them first.
# 10 · ACT PROMPTLY
8 | Remember: discharge asks whether the case should proceed. The charge identifies what must be answered. Neither is a finding of guilt.
6 | Your checklist: check the case type, record the correct deadline trigger, and raise your discharge grounds promptly.
6 | Court moving to frame the charge too early? Ask for time, and put your objection on record.
