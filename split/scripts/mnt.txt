// STATUTE (Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, Chapter X "Order for maintenance of wives, children and parents"):
//  - s.144(1) (c03, c05-c08): "If any person having sufficient means neglects or refuses to maintain- (a) his wife, unable to
//    maintain herself; or (b) his legitimate or illegitimate child, whether married or not, unable to maintain itself; or (c) his
//    legitimate or illegitimate child (not being a married daughter) who has attained majority, where such child is, by reason of any
//    physical or mental abnormality or injury unable to maintain itself; or (d) his father or mother, unable to maintain himself or
//    herself, a Magistrate of the first class may, upon proof of such neglect or refusal, order such person to make a monthly
//    allowance for the maintenance of his wife or such child, father or mother, at such monthly rate as such Magistrate thinks fit".
//    Successor of s.125 CrPC (Indian Kanoon marks it "Similar to Section 125"). "Magistrate of the first class": panel only.
//  - s.144(1)(b) DROPS THE WORD "MINOR" (c06, c09). s.125(1)(b) CrPC read "his legitimate or illegitimate minor child, whether married
//    or not"; the first proviso also dropped "minor" ("the father of a female child referred to in clause (b) ... until she attains
//    her majority, if ... the husband of such female child, if married, is not possessed of sufficient means" - panel only). Checked
//    in the Gazette PDF itself. Effect UNSETTLED: LiveLaw commentary ("Maintenance Under BNSS, Omission Of Word 'Minor', A Major
//    Change"; "Reading Between Lines: Interpretive Analysis Of Section 144 BNSS") reads it as widening; the Kerala High Court (Nov 2025,
//    as reported by LiveLaw) said the scheme of s.125 CrPC / s.144 BNSS does not contemplate a major daughter's claim; Amarendra Kumar
//    Paul v. Maya Paul, (2009) 8 SCC 359 (old Code) limited (b) to minors; no Supreme Court ruling on s.144 found. Hence c09 "not yet
//    settled by the courts" - never show adult children under (b) as entitled.
//  - "his" covers a daughter (c08): BNSS s.2(2) adopts BNS definitions; BNS s.2(10) "The pronoun 'he' and its derivatives are used of
//    any person, whether male, female or transgender" (BNS, Indian Kanoon doc 149679501) - same reasoning as Vijaya Manohar Arbat.
//  - s.144(1) Explanation (c10): "For the purposes of this Chapter, 'wife' includes a woman who has been divorced by, or has obtained a
//    divorce from, her husband and has not remarried."
//  - No ceiling (c15): s.125(1) once capped the allowance at "not exceeding five hundred rupees in the whole"; the Code of Criminal
//    Procedure (Amendment) Act, 2001 (Act 50 of 2001, 24 Sep 2001; text read on Casemine's copy) omitted those words and inserted the
//    interim-maintenance and sixty-day provisos. s.144 BNSS: "at such monthly rate as such Magistrate thinks fit" (c16).
//  - s.144(1) second proviso (c18): "during the pendency of the proceeding" the Magistrate may order "a monthly allowance for the
//    interim maintenance ... and the expenses of such proceeding which the Magistrate considers reasonable"; third proviso (c18): the
//    application "shall, as far as possible, be disposed of within sixty days from the date of the service of notice of the
//    application to such person".
//  - s.144(2) (c19): "payable from the date of the order, or, if so ordered, from the date of the application for maintenance or
//    interim maintenance and expenses of proceeding, as the case may be".
//  - s.144(3) (c27-c29): fails "without sufficient cause to comply" -> "for every breach of the order, issue a warrant for levying the
//    amount due in the manner provided for levying fines" ("as fines": panel only), and may sentence him "for the whole or any part of
//    each month's allowance ... remaining unpaid after the execution of the warrant, to imprisonment for a term which may extend to one
//    month or until payment if sooner made"; first proviso: no warrant "unless application be made to the Court to levy such amount
//    within a period of one year from the date on which it became due". Second proviso (c25): offer to maintain on condition she lives
//    with him, she refuses -> the Magistrate "may consider any grounds of refusal stated by her, and may make an order ...
//    notwithstanding such offer, if he is satisfied that there is just ground for so doing". Explanation (c26): "If a husband has
//    contracted marriage with another woman or keeps a mistress, it shall be considered to be just ground for his wife's refusal to
//    live with him" (c26 speaks the first limb only; the panel may show both).
//  - s.144(4) (c23): no wife entitled "if she is living in adultery, or if, without any sufficient reason, she refuses to live with her
//    husband, or if they are living separately by mutual consent". s.144(5) (c24): on proof of any of these "the Magistrate shall
//    cancel the order". These bars apply to a WIFE only - not to children or parents.
//  - s.145(1) (c31, c32): proceedings "may be taken against any person in any district- (a) where he is; or (b) where he or his wife
//    resides; or (c) where he last resided with his wife, or as the case may be, with the mother of the illegitimate child; or (d)
//    where his father or mother resides". (d) is NEW (s.126(1) CrPC had only (a)-(c)). The text says "where he or his wife resides",
//    NOT "where the claimant resides" - the panel must not paraphrase it so. (c) for the mother of an illegitimate child: panel only.
//  - Family Courts Act, 1984, s.7(2)(a) (c33): a Family Court "shall also have and exercise the jurisdiction exercisable by a

// CASES (Supreme Court; court copies read on Indian Kanoon on 10-11-2026):

# 1 · MAINTENANCE: A RIGHT, NOT CHARITY
8 | Can a wife who earns a salary still claim maintenance? Can parents claim from a married daughter? Does jail wipe out arrears?
8 | Hi, I'm Ravishankar from LawWorld. Who can claim maintenance, how much can be ordered, and how is the order enforced?
10 | The law is Section One Forty Four of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, or B N S S, which replaces Section One Twenty Five of the old Code.
10 | Under the old Code, Chaturbhuj versus Sita Bai calls it a measure of social justice. Its object is to prevent vagrancy and destitution.
# 2 · WHO CAN CLAIM
8 | So who can claim? Four groups of relatives, against a person with sufficient means who neglects or refuses to maintain them.
8 | First, a wife unable to maintain herself. Second, a child, legitimate or illegitimate, unable to maintain itself.
10 | Third, an adult child, other than a married daughter, unable to maintain itself because of a physical or mental abnormality or injury.
10 | Fourth, a parent unable to maintain themselves. Vijaya Manohar Arbat, under the old Code, holds that parents may claim from a daughter with means of her own.
8 | Unlike the old Code, clause B no longer uses the word minor. Whether that helps adult children is not yet settled by the courts. || Unlike the old Code, clause (b) no longer uses the word “minor”. Whether that helps adult children is not yet settled by the courts.
# 3 · WHO COUNTS AS A WIFE
6 | For this Chapter, wife also includes a divorced woman who has not remarried. || For this Chapter, “wife” also includes a divorced woman who has not remarried.
8 | The remedy is secular. The Supreme Court has held, under the old Code, that it covers women of every faith.
# 4 · HYPOTHETICAL: DEEPA'S CLAIM
8 | Here is a hypothetical. Deepa and her young son live with her parents. Her husband Raghu, an engineer, has stopped paying for them.
8 | Deepa earns a little from tutoring. Under Chaturbhuj, that alone does not end her claim. Can she live as she did in her husband's home?
8 | In Anju Garg, under the old Code, the Supreme Court said an able bodied husband must earn, even by physical labour. || In Anju Garg, under the old Code, the Supreme Court said an able-bodied husband must earn, even by physical labour.
# 5 · HOW MUCH, AND INTERIM RELIEF
8 | How much? There is no statutory ceiling. The old limit of five hundred rupees a month was removed in two thousand one. || How much? There is no statutory ceiling. The old limit of ₹500 a month was removed in 2001.
8 | The Magistrate fixes a monthly rate, weighing status, reasonable needs, independent income and the standard of living.
8 | Rajnesh versus Neha, under the old Code, says it must neither oppress the payer, nor be so meagre that it drives the wife to penury.
8 | Pending the case, interim maintenance and expenses may be ordered, as far as possible within sixty days of notice.
# 6 · FROM WHEN, AND FULL DISCLOSURE
8 | Sub section Two: the allowance runs from the date of the order or, if the court so orders, from the date of the application.
8 | Rajnesh directed that maintenance be awarded from the date of application, since delays are beyond the applicant's control.
10 | It also made a disclosure affidavit of assets and liabilities mandatory for both sides. False statements in it may invite perjury proceedings.
8 | Claiming under another law too, like the Domestic Violence Act? Disclose that order. The court will consider adjusting the amounts.
# 7 · WHEN A WIFE CANNOT CLAIM
10 | Sub section Four bars a wife who lives in adultery, refuses without sufficient reason to live with her husband, or lives apart by mutual consent.
6 | Proof of any of these also requires the Magistrate to cancel an existing order.
8 | Suppose Raghu offers maintenance only if Deepa returns. The Magistrate may weigh her reasons, and still order it on just ground.
6 | The law itself treats his marrying another woman as a just ground for her refusal.
# 8 · ENFORCING THE ORDER
8 | If Raghu fails without sufficient cause to pay, the Magistrate may issue a warrant, for every breach, to levy the amount due.
8 | For each month's allowance left unpaid after the warrant, he may be imprisoned for up to one month, or until he pays, if sooner.
8 | But there is a time limit. Deepa must apply to levy the amount within one year of the date on which it became due.
8 | Jail does not wipe out the debt. Kuldip Kaur, under the old Code, calls imprisonment a mode of enforcement, not satisfaction.
# 9 · WHERE TO FILE, AND LATER CHANGES
8 | Section One Forty Five lets Deepa file where Raghu is, where he or his wife resides, or where he last resided with his wife.
6 | The B N S S adds a new option: the district where his father or mother resides.
6 | Where a Family Court has been set up for the area, it exercises this jurisdiction.
// c34 reworded 10-11 20:3x: Flow's policy filter refused the line with "alter the allowance, up or down". Same meaning (s.146(1)).
8 | Under Section One Forty Six, the Magistrate may alter the order on proof of a change in the circumstances of either party.
# 10 · YOUR CHECKLIST
8 | Finally, a checklist. Gather proof of the relationship, the respondent's income and assets, and your own monthly expenses.
10 | File the disclosure affidavit with your application, and seek interim maintenance early. If payments stop, apply within one year of each missed payment.
8 | Maintenance prevents destitution; it is not a penalty. The amount always depends on the evidence before the court.
