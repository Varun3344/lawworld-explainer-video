# 1 · CAN A STRANGER APPEAL?
10 | Hi, I am Ravishankar from LawWorld. No appeal lies from a consent decree. But does that bar stop a stranger to the compromise, whose rights the decree affects?

# 3 · THE CHALLENGE
10 | The appellant argued that Section Ninety Six, sub section Three, bars any appeal from a consent decree, and that a stranger to the compromise cannot challenge it. || The appellant argued that Section 96(3) bars any appeal from a consent decree, and that a stranger to the compromise cannot challenge it.
# 4 · WHAT THE COURT HELD
10 | The Court disagreed. That bar, and the estoppel under Order Twenty Three, bind only the parties to the compromise. A stranger is not deemed to have consented. || The Court disagreed. That bar, and the estoppel under Order XXIII, bind only the parties to the compromise. A stranger is not deemed to have consented.
10 | But a stranger needs leave, and must show prima facie prejudice. A consent decree faces no stricter test. Here, leave was rightly granted, with no view on the merits.
# 5 · THE VERDICT
10 | A stranger's knowledge can't be assumed from the decree's date; with the delay explained, condoning it was no error. The injunction wasn't disturbed; the appeals failed.
