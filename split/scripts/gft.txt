// Facts used (para): revision under Article 227 by the defendants to strike off the plaint in O.S. 756/2020, Principal District
// Munsif Court, Puducherry (prayer, 1). The plaintiff sued for a permanent injunction against interference with her possession,
// claiming ownership under a sale deed dated 27.1.2012 (4). The owner (the first petitioner's father) sued his tenant for ejectment
// in O.S. 945/1982, decreed 11.11.1983; reversed in A.S. 25/1984 (22.12.1987); this Court restored the decree on 18.8.2000 in S.A.
// 1441/1988; the Supreme Court confirmed it in C.A. 5384/2001 on 23.11.2005, granting the tenant time till 31.8.2006 to vacate (5(a)).
// The tenant and his wife executed a registered settlement deed dated 10.8.2001 in favour of their son, who executed two registered
// sale deeds, one to the plaintiff and one to another buyer; on the owner's heirs' application, the District Registrar cancelled the
// plaintiff's sale deed on 11.12.2015 (5(b)). The plaintiff's W.P. 5723/2014 against a Tahsildar's order of 11.2.2014 was dismissed
// on 05.01.2016 (5(c), (d)); the other buyer's W.P. 12168/2014 was dismissed on 21.4.2023 with a direction to vacate (5(e), (f), 10).
// O.S. 756/2020 filed for an injunction; this C.R.P. seeks to strike it off (5(g)). Defendants: the plaintiff concealed all the
// earlier facts; abuse of process; an attempt to get over the earlier decrees and writ orders (6). Plaintiff: P. Suresh v. D.
// Kalaivani, 2026 (7) SCC 644 - a petition under Article 227 cannot be entertained to strike off a plaint (7); P. Suresh, paras 37-40:
// Article 227 "has to be treated as an exceptional resort when an alternative efficacious civil remedy ... like Order VII Rule 11, CPC
// ... is available" (8, 9). Held: "a text book case" (10); the plaintiff, "apart from concealing all the earlier proceedings upto the
// Hon'ble Apex Court and the orders passed in the earlier writ petitions, has indulged in abuse of process of court and used the Court
// to perpetuate illegality"; the tenant's settlement deed and the son's sale deeds "would amount to contumacious conduct and would
// tantamount to contempt of court" (11); the owner "has been made to run from pillar to post till date" despite succeeding up to the
// Supreme Court in 2005; the suit "is a clear abuse of process of court and a re-litigation, which are not grounds that can be raised
// under Order VII Rule 11 of the CPC"; a fit case to exercise Article 227 "in order to prevent further miscarriage of justice" (12);
// the plea that the property is different is unsustainable - she claims through the same settlement deed and sale deeds (13-15).
// ORDER: C.R.P. allowed; O.S. 756/2020 struck off the register; no costs; C.M.P.s closed (16).
// Not said: any person's name (the tenant, his son, the plaintiff, the other buyer, the owner and his heirs are private persons; the
