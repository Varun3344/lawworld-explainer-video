// Facts used: the Revenue appealed against orders holding against it (2); Orissa Mining Corporation Ltd.: AY 2008-09, 2010-11 and
// 2011-12; an individual assessee (respondent in the connected appeals, not named): AY 2009-10 and 2010-11 (2); returns under s.139,
// assessed, orders under s.143(3); reopened under s.147, notices under s.148 to file returns in the prescribed form (4); the company
// asked that its original s.139 return be treated as its return in response to the s.148 notice; the individual refiled the same
// return (5); no notice under s.143(2) was issued; s.142(1) notices sought documents / stock information (6); reassessment orders,
// appealed to the CIT(A) on the missing s.143(2) notice (7); CIT(A): having participated after the s.148 and s.142(1) notices, the
// assessees could not raise it at the appellate stage (8); ITAT: proceedings vitiated for want of the s.143(2) notice, reassessment
// orders set aside (9); the High Court rejected the Revenue's challenge and upheld the ITAT (10). The Revenue's case: the notice was
// not required, its omission not fatal; reliance on s.292BB (14). Held: s.143(2) notice mandatory, the chance to produce evidence;
// a "sine qua non" for reassessment under s.147; without it "no further proceedings can be undertaken" (12, 13); Hotel Blue Moon
// (2010) 3 SCC 259: the omission is not a curable procedural irregularity, the requirement cannot be dispensed with (12, 20);
// s.292BB deems a notice duly served, in time and properly, where the assessee appeared / co-operated; it comes into play "only if
// notice is alleged to have been sent", not where no notice was issued (15, 16); mere participation confers no jurisdiction (17);
// Laxman Das Khandelwal (2021) 18 SCC 691: s.292BB "does not save complete absence of notice" (21); reiterating the original return
// does not dispense with the s.143(2) notice (18); a s.142(1) letter is no substitute, "would not validate what is non est" (22); no
// infirmity in the ITAT's view as affirmed by the High Court; the three questions answered against the Revenue (23); appeals
// dismissed (24). Assessment years are exact on the cards; in speech: "three assessment years, between 2008 and 2012".

# 3 · THE APPEALS
10 | Reassessment orders followed. The appellate commissioner said: you took part, too late to object. But the Tribunal set them aside, and the High Court agreed.
10 | The Revenue appealed. It said the notice was not needed, relying on Section Two Ninety Two B B: having taken part, the assessees could not object. || The Revenue appealed. It said the notice was not needed, relying on Section 292BB: having taken part, the assessees could not object.
# 4 · WHAT THE COURT HELD
10 | The Court held: this notice is a sine qua non for reassessment. Following Hotel Blue Moon, its absence is not a mere irregularity, and cannot be cured.
10 | Section Two Ninety Two B B deems a sent notice duly served, once the assessee takes part. It cannot save a notice that was never issued at all. || Section 292BB deems a sent notice duly served, once the assessee takes part. It cannot save a notice that was never issued at all.
# 5 · THE VERDICT
10 | Standing by the old return does not excuse the notice, and a Section One Forty Two letter is no substitute. The Revenue's appeals were dismissed. || Standing by the old return does not excuse the notice, and a Section 142 letter is no substitute. The Revenue's appeals were dismissed.
