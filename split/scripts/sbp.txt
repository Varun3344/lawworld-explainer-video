// Facts used: appellant = distribution licensee in Bihar (5); a tripartite Distribution Franchisee Agreement dated 31.12.2013 with the
// successful bidder (India Power Corporation Ltd.) and its special purpose vehicle (5.1); clause 17.2.5: unresolved disputes to
// arbitration "by a panel consisting of three arbitrators" (14.1, 14.2); arbitration notice 09.11.2016 (5.3); on the SPV's Section 11
// request, the Patna High Court, with the parties' consent, appointed a retired judge of that court as sole arbitrator, 04.04.2018
// (5.3); counter claim 02.02.2019 + supplementary counter claim 28.03.2019 (5.4); NCLT admitted insolvency (CIRP) against the SPV,
// respondent No. 2, 08.11.2019 (5.5); again with consent, the High Court appointed a former Chief Justice of India as the new
// arbitrator, 10.03.2021 (5.6); procedural order 07.04.2021: no objection to the composition (5.7, 21.2); one year, then six months
// more by consent; before that ran out (07.08.2021) the respondents asked the High Court for four more months (Request Case No. 36 of 2021) (5.7, 15);
// the appellant objected: no jurisdiction to extend time, a three-member tribunal per the agreement (Request Case No. 78 of 2022),
// and the SPV in insolvency had no locus to arbitrate (5.8, 5.11); High Court, 15.09.2023: consent = waiver, conduct = acquiescence,
// locus upheld, and it had jurisdiction under Section 29A: mandate extended by four months (3, 5.11).
// Held: (1) by its conduct the appellant "clearly waived its right to object to the composition of the arbitral tribunal"; took part,
// consented to the extension, filed a counter claim and a supplementary one = "clear acquiescence"; not open to it "at the very
// belated stage" (25); Sections 4 and 16(2) (23, 24); the two appointment orders never challenged, final and binding (22); Pam
// Development (2014) 11 SCC 366 (26); High Court affirmed (27). (2) Section 14 IBC moratorium bars debt recovery action against the
// corporate debtor; "it would not impair any claim by the corporate debtor, such as, an arbitral claim, which if successful, would
// only bolster the financial health thereby enhancing the value of the corporate debtor" (29); resolution professional authorised
// the signatories, approved by the committee of creditors, Section 28(1)(h) IBC (30); affirmed (31). (3) "court" = Section 2(1)(e)(i):
// the principal civil court of original jurisdiction in a district, including a High Court in its ordinary original civil
// jurisdiction; Section 11 is about appointment only, "nothing more should be read into it" (34); Associated Contractors (2015) 1 SCC
// 32 (37); BSC & C and C JV, 13.05.2024 (38); Jagdeep Chowgule (2026) 5 SCC 667: Section 11 jurisdiction "stands exhausted upon the

# 2 · THE ARBITRATION
10 | South Bihar Power Distribution Company's franchisee contract required three arbitrators. By consent, the Patna High Court appointed a sole arbitrator.
10 | Later, by consent, a former Chief Justice of India took over. Before one year, plus six months by consent, ran out, the respondents asked the High Court for four more months.
# 3 · THE OBJECTIONS
10 | The appellant objected: three arbitrators, the claimant's insolvency, and the High Court's power to extend time. The High Court rejected every one of them.
# 4 · WHAT THE COURT HELD
10 | On the panel, the Court held: having consented twice, taken part, and filed a counter claim, the appellant waived its objection under Section Four. It came too late. || On the panel, the Court held: having consented twice, taken part, and filed a counter claim, the appellant waived its objection under Section 4. It came too late.
10 | And insolvency was no bar. The moratorium stops recovery against the debtor, not the debtor's own claim, which, if it succeeds, only adds to its value.
10 | But acting under Section Eleven, the High Court could not extend time. That role ends once the tribunal is formed. Only the court defined in Section Two can. || But acting under Section 11, the High Court could not extend time. That role ends once the tribunal is formed. Only the court defined in Section 2 can.
# 5 · THE VERDICT
10 | Since the Patna Commercial Court had already extended the mandate, the error made no difference. The appeals were dismissed, and the award must come within six weeks.
