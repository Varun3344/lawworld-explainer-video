// Facts used (para): revision under s.91 of the Telangana Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act, 1950 against the Joint Collector,
// Mahabubnagar's order 01.09.2012 (Case F1/IA-05/2010), which dismissed the s.90 appeal and confirmed the RDO, Mahabubnagar's order
// 30.03.2010 (File K/1972/2009) issuing s.38-E certificates to all the legal heirs of the late protected tenant, Ac. 8.29 guntas in
// Sy. No. 456, Yelikatta village, Farooqnagar mandal (1). He was the protected tenant; the final list of protected tenants named him, but
// his s.38-E certificate stayed incomplete for want of an application (3, 4). He died in 1980-81 leaving five sons and five daughters;
// all except one son have since died; petitioners 1-3 are that son's children, the respondents the other grandchildren (5). In 2007
// that son, "the only surviving son", applied claiming exclusive possession; RDO's proceedings 04.10.2007 registered him as owner and
// issued a Form-II certificate (6). Within about two months he sold Ac. 2.09 guntas (13.12.2007) to petitioner 4 and Ac. 6.20 guntas
// (20.12.2007) to petitioner 5 and others (7). The other heirs appealed under s.90; the Joint Collector on 04.06.2009 set aside the
// 04.10.2007 order and remanded for a de novo enquiry (8). After notice to all the legal heirs, the RDO on 30.03.2010 directed the
// certificate in favour of all the legal heirs "with equal rights in accordance with their respective lineal shares"; the Joint
// Collector dismissed the petitioners' appeal on 01.09.2012 (9, 10: s.40, all lineal descendants succeed). Petitioners: a family
// arrangement after the death gave the land to that son, who stayed in continuous possession; the s.48-A bar runs from acquisition,
// not the certificate; revenue authorities cannot decide disputed succession - Civil Court (12-17). Respondents: s.40, all lineal
// descendants succeed; the 2007 certificate was issued without notice to them (18-22). Framework: the Act protects tenants of
// agricultural lands; s.38-E: ownership of land held by a protected tenant stands transferred to him on the notified date, 01.01.1973,
// and the certificate is conclusive evidence of ownership; s.40 makes the rights heritable, lineal descendants the heirs (28). The
// certificate "evidences ownership that has already vested; it is not the source of that ownership" (33). Point (i): the s.48-A
// eight-year bar runs from the notified date, 01.01.1973, not from the certificate (34, 35, 49(a)). Point (ii): s.40 - "upon the death
// of a protected tenant, his lineal descendants by blood are entitled to hold the land on the same terms" (36); with no serious dispute
// about heirship the revenue authorities may recognise succession (41-44, 49(b)); here neither the son nor his children ever disputed
// the respondents' relationship; his was "a claim to exclusive rights over the land and not a dispute about the status of the
// respondents as lineal descendants" (43). Point (iii): ownership does not depend on possession (Sada, AIR 1988 AP 77 FB; Thota
// Sridhar Reddy, 2021 INSC 588); the heirs' entitlement "flows from Section 40 and not from their possession"; a co-owner cannot claim
// exclusive possession against the others; the respondents "are entitled to equal shares as per the lineage" (45); the family
// arrangement and sale deeds are private matters, no opinion expressed (46). Point (iv): the RDO and Joint Collector's approach is in
// accordance with law; no illegality, material irregularity or error of jurisdiction (47, 48). Conclusion and RESULT: the son was not
// entitled to the certificate "to the exclusion of the other legal heirs ... who are entitled to equal shares as per their lineage"
// (49(c)); validity of the sale deeds and the family arrangement left open (50); C.R.P. dismissed; the Joint Collector's order
// affirming the RDO's confirmed; the s.38-E certificate to the legal heirs, with equal shares as per their lineage, confirmed; no
// costs (51, 52).
