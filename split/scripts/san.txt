// Facts used: appeal by the complainant against the acquittal by the JFCM, Varkala in ST No. 2754 of 2015 (judgment 24.09.2024) (para 1);
// the accused borrowed Rs 9,30,230 through various transactions / bank transfers; Ext.P1 cheque for Rs 9,30,230 dated 06.07.2015 (handed
// over 02.07.2015) towards repayment; returned "funds insufficient"; legal notice, no payment; s.138 NI Act (paras 4, 10, 11); the
// Magistrate found the transaction, the cheque and the failure to pay for the complainant (6, 12), but on DW2 and Ext.D5 (the accused's
// statement of accounts) the money went out in instalments in 2011-2012, so the cheque was for a time-barred debt, "not a legally
// enforceable debt" for s.138, and acquitted on that sole reason (6, 12); the complainant does not dispute 2011-2012 (7, 12) and relies
// on Ramakrishnan v. Parthasaradhy, 2003 (2) KLT 613 (DB) para 25: "When a person issues a cheque, he acknowledges his liability to pay"
// (7); the State Brief for the accused: a time-barred debt is not legally enforceable on first principle, the position needs
// reconsideration (2, 8). Held: a cheque for a time-barred debt "is akin to revival of the debt ... by acknowledging the debt and
// liability", the debt ceases to be time-barred; issuing a cheque for a time-barred debt "is not legally prohibited"; that alone "does
// not make a prosecution under Section 138 ... impermissible"; the Magistrate went wrong (13); appeal allowed, acquittal set aside,
// convicted under s.138 (14); simple imprisonment till rising of the Court, fine Rs 14,00,000, default six months, Rs 13,50,000

# 4 · THE ARGUMENTS
10 | On appeal, the complainant accepted those years, but relied on a Division Bench ruling: when a person issues a cheque, he acknowledges his liability to pay.
10 | Counsel appointed for the accused replied that a time-barred debt is not legally enforceable, and that the position needed reconsideration.
# 5 · WHAT THE COURT HELD
10 | The High Court held: a cheque issued for a time-barred debt acknowledges that debt, and is akin to reviving it. So the debt ceases to be time-barred.
10 | Issuing a cheque for a time-barred debt is not legally prohibited. That alone does not make a Section One Thirty Eight prosecution impermissible. || Issuing a cheque for a time-barred debt is not legally prohibited. That alone does not make a Section 138 prosecution impermissible.
# 6 · THE VERDICT
10 | The acquittal was set aside, and the accused was convicted. The sentence: simple imprisonment till the Court rises, and a fine of fourteen lakh rupees. || The acquittal was set aside, and the accused was convicted. The sentence: simple imprisonment till the Court rises, and a fine of ₹14 lakh.
