# 1 · FOUR CLOCKS AFTER A BOUNCED CHEQUE
8 | Your cheque has bounced. So when must you send notice, how long does the drawer get to pay, and when can you file a complaint?
10 | Hi, I'm Ravishankar from LawWorld. A complaint under Section One Thirty Eight of the Negotiable Instruments Act runs on four separate legal clocks.
# 2 · THE POSITION OF LAW
10 | First, the essentials. The bounced cheque must relate to a legally enforceable debt or liability, and the statutory conditions must be met.
10 | In Rangappa versus Sri Mohan, the Supreme Court confirmed a rebuttable presumption for the holder, once the necessary foundation is established.
6 | That foundation includes the drawer's signature. The drawer can still raise a probable defence.
8 | A security cheque may also attract liability, when the secured debt has become payable and remains unpaid.
# 3 · CLOCK ONE: PRESENT IN TIME
8 | Clock one starts with the cheque's date. The Act itself mentions six months, or the cheque's validity, whichever is earlier.
10 | R B I directions limit ordinary cheque validity to three months. Do not assume ninety days. A postdated cheque becomes payable on its stated date. || RBI directions limit ordinary cheque validity to three months. Do not assume 90 days. A postdated cheque becomes payable on its stated date.
10 | Deposit early enough to reach the drawee bank in time. Ishar Alloy explains why a last minute deposit at your collecting bank may be insufficient. || Deposit early enough to reach the drawee bank in time. Ishar Alloy explains why a last-minute deposit at your collecting bank may be insufficient.
# 4 · CLOCK TWO: THE DEMAND NOTICE
10 | Clock two begins when the payee receives the bank's information that the cheque was returned unpaid. Within thirty days, give the drawer a written demand notice.
8 | Record both the memo date, and when the information actually reached you. Clearly identify the cheque, and demand its amount.
8 | If interest or costs are claimed, state them separately. Suman Sethi explains why the cheque amount must be identifiable.
10 | Dispatch and receipt dates serve different purposes. The payment clock turns on receipt, including legally presumed service where applicable.
8 | C C Alavi Haji explains the rebuttable presumption for a correctly addressed notice sent by registered post. || C.C. Alavi Haji explains the rebuttable presumption for a correctly addressed notice sent by registered post.
10 | Refusal or avoidance does not necessarily defeat service. Keep the signed notice, postal records and address proof, and never invent a delivery date.
# 5 · CLOCK THREE: THE PAYMENT WINDOW
8 | Clock three gives the drawer fifteen days after receipt of the notice to pay the cheque amount. Exclude the receipt date when counting.
10 | Payment within this period prevents the cause of action from arising on that notice. A reply denying liability is not payment, and it does not stop the clock.
8 | There is no separate statutory reply deadline. A drawer who disputes the demand should still reply promptly, with records.
10 | If unpaid after those fifteen days, the cause of action arises on the next day. Do not file early, even if the drawer has rejected the demand.
10 | In Yogendra Pratap Singh versus Savitri Pandey, the Supreme Court held that filing before the payment period expires is not maintainable.
6 | Later cognizance cannot cure a premature complaint; a fresh one may need delay condoned.
# 6 · CLOCK FOUR: FILE WITHIN ONE MONTH
10 | Clock four is one calendar month from the cause of action, not a fixed thirty days. Econ Antri confirms that the cause of action date is excluded.
8 | A delayed complaint may be entertained if the court accepts sufficient cause under the proviso to Section One Forty Two.
10 | But a delayed notice is different; this proviso does not excuse it. Negotiations and promises to pay do not, by themselves, extend the filing deadline.
# 7 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
10 | Here is a fictional example. The cheque was presented within validity, and the bank's dishonour information reached the payee on first May. || Here is a fictional example. The cheque was presented within validity, and the bank's dishonour information reached the payee on 1 May.
8 | Notice was dispatched on second May, and actually received on sixth May. So the payment days run from seventh to twenty first May. || Notice was dispatched on 2 May, and actually received on 6 May. So the payment days run from 7 to 21 May.
8 | If unpaid, the cause of action arises on twenty second May, and the ordinary complaint window runs to twenty second June. || If unpaid, the cause of action arises on 22 May, and the ordinary complaint window runs to 22 June.
8 | These dates assume proven actual receipt, no intervening payment, and no special court closure or extension issue.
# 8 · THE COURT AND THE COMPLAINT
8 | Now file a written complaint. It must be made by the payee or holder in due course, before the competent Magistrate.
10 | For collection through an account, jurisdiction ordinarily follows the payee's account branch, even if deposited at another branch of that bank.
10 | Presentation otherwise than through an account follows the drawer's bank branch. Also check the special rule for subsequent complaints against the same drawer.
10 | Organise the cheque, return memo, debt records, notice, service proof and date calculation, together with the required synopsis and affidavits.
# 9 · COMMON MISTAKES
8 | Avoid treating every return memo as automatic guilt, confusing a reply with payment, or filing early.
10 | A cheque may be presented again within its validity. M S R Leathers permits prosecution on a subsequent dishonour, if all conditions are met. || A cheque may be presented again within its validity. MSR Leathers permits prosecution on a subsequent dishonour, if all conditions are met.
6 | But a reminder notice alone, without a fresh dishonour, does not restart the clock.
10 | After the payment window, document any later payment. It does not automatically erase the case; settlement or compounding needs legal steps.
# 10 · REMEMBER THE SEQUENCE
10 | Remember the sequence. Present within the cheque's validity, ordinarily three months. Send notice within thirty days of receiving the bank's information.
10 | Allow fifteen days after notice receipt for payment. Then file within one calendar month of the cause of action. And keep proof for every date.
8 | Those four clocks, supported by the underlying debt and proper service, form the foundation of a cheque dishonour complaint.
