// Facts used (para): intra-court appeal under Clause 15 of the Letters Patent by the State against the interlocutory order dated
// 30.10.2026 of the Single Judge in W.M.P. No. 50214 of 2026 in W.P. No. 44715 of 2026 (prayer, 1); the Single Judge granted an
// "omnibus stay" of G.O. (Ms.) No. 181, Municipal Administration and Water Supply (ME.3) Department, dated 09.10.2026, on the
// restructuring and execution of Solid Waste Management operations under "Category II" across 147 Urban Local Bodies (1). Issue:
// whether the Single Judge was justified in an expansive interim order that effectively granted the final relief, halting a major
// executive policy framework on public health and municipal conservancy on the eve of the expiry of transitional contract extensions,
// without satisfying the tests for interlocutory orders (2). Advocate General: the policy is in the executive domain and the Urban
// Local Bodies' competence, Article 243W with Entry 6 of the Twelfth Schedule (3.1); the G.O. is for compliance with the Solid Waste
// Management Rules under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; the new hybrid direct-management model uses direct electronic bank
// transfers, biometric attendance and direct statutory remittances (3.2); contractors have no vested right to perpetual renewals or to
// insist on outsourcing (Erusian Equipment, (1975) 1 SCC 70; Association of Registration Plates, (2005) 1 SCC 679) (3.3); the
// association represents only seven private operators with no subsisting contract with any ULB; a Rs.616.28 crore framework across
// 147 ULBs, 140 of them with zero subsisting contracts (3.4, the State's submission); balance of convenience with the State; the stay
// threatened "complete sanitation paralysis" (3.5). Respondent: G.O. 181 is arbitrary, Article 14, splitting the ULBs into Category I
// and Category II "without any intelligible differentia" (4.2); under the 2022 policy contractors invested in compactor trucks, hook
// loaders, battery-operated vehicles and sorting equipment; legitimate expectation; Article 19(1)(g) (4.3); the writ appeal is not
// maintainable against an ad-interim order, the State ought to have sought to vacate the stay before the Single Judge (4.4). HELD: an
// interlocutory order with the characteristics of a "judgment" is appealable (5); Midnapore Peoples' Coop. Bank v. Chunilal Nanda,
// (2006) 5 SCC 399, five categories (6); this interim order is not routine (categories iv/v): "by paralyzing essential municipal
// sanitation operations across 147 urban centres and altering vital legal rights", it is a "judgment" open to appeal under Clause 15
// (7); N.G. Projects, (2022) 6 SCC 127, and NHSRCL v. Montecarlo, (2022) 6 SCC 401: courts must refrain from mechanical interim stays
// halting essential public works or utilities; "When private commercial expectations are pitted against overwhelming public interest,
// public interest must decisively prevail."; here the balance of convenience lay "overwhelmingly in favour of the State" (8); the
// interlocutory order of 30.10.2026 set aside in the larger public interest (9). ORDER: writ appeal allowed, the order of 30.10.2026
// set aside, "full liberty to implement G.O. (Ms.) No.181", no costs, C.M.P. closed (10); all observations "strictly prima facie",
// confined to the interlocutory relief; the merits to be decided independently by the Single Judge; counter-affidavits in three
// weeks; the Single Judge requested to expedite W.P. No. 44715 of 2026 (closing paragraph after 10).
