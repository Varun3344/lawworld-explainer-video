// Facts used (para): the insurer (R3 before the Tribunal) appeals the Tribunal's order and decree of 25.07.2017 granting Rs.9,14,800 for
// the death of a young man in a motor accident of 12.02.2011 (1); parties referred to as arrayed before the Tribunal: "petitioners" =
// the claimants (his parents), "respondents" = R1 driver, R2 owner, R3 insurer of the tractor and trailer (2, 3). Claimants' case: on
// 12.02.2011, about 8.30 PM, he was driving a car from Palasa to Srikakulam; near Akula Nandigam village a tractor and trailer came on
// the wrong route rashly and negligently and dashed against the car; he died; aged about 24, a male nursing orderly earning Rs.9,600 a
// month; Crime No.14 of 2011 (Ss. 304-A, 338 IPC) against the tractor driver; R1-R3 jointly and severally liable (3). R3's written
// statement: the deceased drove rashly and negligently, the vehicle had no valid fitness certificate, policy breach, excessive claim (4).
// Tribunal: Rs.9,14,800 with interest at 9% a year (6). Appeal grounds: no negligence of R1 / the deceased negligent, no fitness
// certificate, contributory negligence, policy violation, salary not properly assessed (6). The claimants filed no appeal or cross
// objections on quantum (9, 11(c)). Point 1: involvement of the tractor undisputed; the only eyewitness PW.2, a co-passenger, testified
// the tractor came on the wrong route and dashed against the car, nothing elicited in cross (10(a)); R3 had only the "self-serving
// statement" of RW.1, its branch manager; R1 ex parte and not examined, "his non examination is fatal" (10(b)); charge sheet against R1;
// MVI report: no mechanical defect; held: the accident occurred due to R1's rash and negligent driving (10(c)). Point 2: age 24 (11(a));
// income Rs.9,600 rightly assessed (11(b)); the claimants' counsel sought just and fair compensation though no appeal / cross objections;
// "learned counsel for the respondents" (as arrayed before the Tribunal) argued that the accident was in 2011 so present principles
// should not apply; held: under Order 41 Rule 33 "this court can enhance the amount of compensation even in the absence of appeal or
// cross objections", Sharanamma, (2013) 11 SCC 517 (11(c)); Susheelamma (AP DB) (11(d)); Surekha v. Santhosh, (2021) 16 SCC 467: no
// hyper-technical approach; "this Court is under obligation to award just and fair compensation" (11(e)); Sarla Verma, Pranay Sethi,
// Magma; Devaraj Medical College: SC decisions apply retrospectively unless expressly prospective (11(f)); the Tribunal "erred in
// considering the age of the mother of the deceased instead of age of the deceased", applied multiplier 11 and, though he was
// unmarried, deducted 1/3 for personal expenses (11(g)); reassessed: Rs.1,15,200 a year, 1/2 deduction, 40% future prospects,
// multiplier 18 = Rs.14,51,520 loss of dependency; filial consortium Rs.40,000 x 2; funeral, transport, loss of estate Rs.15,000 each;
// total Rs.15,76,520, rounded to Rs.15,76,500 (11(h)), "just and reasonable" (11(i)). Liability: RW.2 - no valid permit on the date;
// breach of the policy, the owner alone liable, but the deceased is a third party, so under Ss. 157(2) and 149 MV Act the insurer pays
// at the first instance and recovers (11(j)-(s)); Challa Upendra Rao, (2004) 8 SCC 517; Amrit Paul Singh, (2018) 7 SCC 558 (11(o),(p)).
// Point 3: the Tribunal erred in determining just and fair compensation; the award is liable to be modified (12). RESULT: appeal
// "allowed in part"; compensation enhanced from Rs.9,14,800 to Rs.15,76,500 with simple interest at 7.5% a year from the date of the
// petition till realisation; Rs.7,88,250 to each claimant; R3 (the insurer) to deposit within one month of the Tribunal receiving the
// judgment, at the first instance, and recover from R1 and R2 (driver and owner); no costs (13).
