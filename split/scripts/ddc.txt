// Facts used (para): the appeal questions her conviction by the Bombay High Court, Aurangabad Bench, under s.302 IPC, rigorous
// imprisonment for life and a fine of Rs.25,000; the HC reversed her acquittal by the Sessions Court (1). Prosecution's case: the
// deceased was married to A1 and lived with his father A2 and mother A3 (the appellant); A4 his younger brother; it was ALLEGED that
// all the accused ill-treated her because she had no issue from the marriage, and that there was a demand of dowry with physical and
// mental torture; it was alleged that on 10.04.2005 at about 10 a.m. all four set her on fire; she was taken to the Civil Hospital
// with burn injuries by Vishwas Desle; her statement recorded at 10.30 a.m. implicated the appellant; further statements by ASI
// Prakash Wagh and the Special Executive Magistrate; she died on 15.04.2005 (2). Charges: ss.498A, 342, 406 and 302 r/w 34 IPC
// against A1-A4 (2, 14). Post-mortem: 90% burns, septicaemia due to thermal burns (3; PW-7 put the burns at 94%, 24).
// The three dying declarations, all on 10.04.2005: Ex-56, the medico-legal case papers, the patient's history recorded by the
// Medical Officer Dr. Prashant Shinde, PW-7 (7, 10, 21, 22), the "initial" one per the HC (10), endorsed by Vishwas Desle (23):
// she was set on fire by the appellant (9). Ex-44, recorded by ASI Prakash Wagh, PW-4 (4; police arrived about 1.40 p.m., 22):
// A1 and A4 tied her hands and legs, the appellant poured kerosene, A2 set her on fire (4, 25). Ex-48, recorded by the Special
// Executive Magistrate, PW-5 (5; about 2.15 p.m., 22): a similar narration (25). Ex-44 and Ex-48: set on fire by her father-in-law
// A2 (9). Sessions Court: the three declarations inconsistent; Vishwas Desle, who took her to hospital, not examined; no neighbours
// examined; no witness to any rope; s.498A not proved on the stamp paper alone; all four acquitted (9). Vishwas Patil (= Desle), a
// Police Patil, put out the fire, was the first at the house, and admitted her to hospital; never examined, no reason given (7, 8,
// 23). High Court (Division Bench): Ex-56 voluntary, without anyone's influence; per the defence of the accused, the appellant
// alone was inside the house and failed to explain the burns; Ex-56 accepted as truthful; Ex-44 and 48 discarded as inconsistent
// with it; the appellant alone convicted under s.302 and sentenced to life; A1, A2, A4's acquittal upheld; ss.498A, 342, 406 not
// proved against anyone; the acquittal held illegal and perverse (10). Appellant: corroboration needed; reversing an acquittal
// needs material without doubt (11). State: Ex-56 voluntary, with the seizures and the Chemical Analyser's report; she was alone
// at home (12).
// Held: the reversal of the acquittal "was not called for" (13); all four were charged only under s.302 r/w 34 (common intention),
// no independent s.302 charge; convicting her alone on the defence statement would be "making out a new accusation", prejudicial
// "in the facts of the present case" (14, 15); Krishna Govind Patil, 1962 INSC 19 (16, 17); she alone was convicted under s.302,
// "a charge that was never framed against her" (18); the Court nonetheless assessed the evidence independently; multiple dying
// declarations: each case on its own facts (Jagbir Singh, 19); the Abhishek Sharma principles, 2023 INSC 924, paras 9.1-9.7 (20);
// the HC based the conviction solely on PW-7 and Ex-56 (21); Vishwas Patil "definitely a material witness" (23); since Ex-44 and
// 48 were inconsistent with Ex-56, the HC "ought to have looked for some corroborative material"; "Exhibit-56 without any
// corroboration cannot be the sole basis to reverse the acquittal of the appellant and thereafter convict her alone" (24); her
// conviction needed convincing evidence; the victim about thirty, the appellant about sixty: a relevant factor in reversing the
// acquittal (25); s.106 Evidence Act only after the prosecution discharges its initial burden (Anees, 2024 INSC 368); not
// discharged here, so the HC's reasoning on her silence is not accepted (26, 27); she was not heard on sentence (s.386(a) CrPC),
// nothing turns on it (28). Para 29: the Sessions Court was correct that "in view of inconsistency in the three dying declarations,
// the appellant was entitled to the benefit of doubt"; the HC, in an appeal against acquittal, ought to have considered this; "The
// presumption of innocence in favour of the appellant was in fact enhanced by her acquittal by the Sessions Court"; the Sessions
// Court's view not perverse or illegal; Ex-56 accepted "without any corroborating evidence being available". Para 30: the HC's
// judgment in Criminal Appeal No. 63 of 2008 dated 28.09.2017 quashed; the appellant acquitted of s.302 IPC; to be set free
// forthwith if not required in any other case; appeal allowed.
// Not said: any private person's name (only the cause title on the cite card); the dowry / ill-treatment allegations only as the

// percentage in speech); the hook does NOT say dying declarations cannot convict: the holding is about these inconsistent
// declarations, no corroboration of Ex-56, and reversing an acquittal. Flow policy: no names, no "admitted" / "fraud" / "collusion".
# 1 · WHICH ONE IS TRUE?
10 | Before she died, a woman gave three statements. One named only her mother-in-law. The other two named all four in the family. Which one would you believe?
10 | The Sessions Court acquitted her husband and his family. The High Court believed just the first statement, and gave the mother-in-law life imprisonment. So who was right?

# 2 · THREE STATEMENTS
10 | The prosecution said her husband and his family ill-treated her over dowry, and because she had no child. She suffered burns at home and died five days later.
10 | A doctor at the hospital recorded the first. A police officer and a Special Executive Magistrate took the other two, which named all four accused.
# 3 · TWO COURTS, TWO VIEWS
10 | The Sessions Court found the statements did not match. The man who put out the fire and took her to hospital was never called as a witness, nor was any neighbour.
10 | The High Court found the doctor's record voluntary. By the family's own defence, it said, the mother-in-law alone was at home with her, and gave no explanation.

# 5 · WHAT THE COURT HELD
10 | Finally, what did the Supreme Court decide? Given the inconsistency in the three statements, the Sessions Court was right. She was entitled to the benefit of doubt.
