# 1 · TWO KINDS OF CUSTODY
10 | Your client is arrested. Police want him for questioning. Will it be police custody, or judicial custody? Who holds him, who authorises it, and for how long?
8 | Under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, or B N S S, these are distinct forms of detention, with safeguards.
# 2 · THE FIRST 24 HOURS
10 | Here is the first deadline. After arrest without warrant, Section Fifty Eight prohibits detention beyond twenty four hours without a Magistrate's authorisation.
8 | Necessary travel time to court is excluded, and police must avoid unnecessary delay. Note the exact arrest time.
8 | Ravi is arrested on Monday morning. Police cannot keep him at the station for days because questioning is unfinished.

# 3 · CONTROL AND PURPOSE
10 | Who holds your client, and why? In police custody, he is entrusted to investigating police, for lawful investigation under the remand order.
10 | That can include lawful questioning, or recovering evidence. Judicial custody is different: the accused is ordinarily kept in prison.
8 | Jail authorities hold him, on the court's order. The investigating officer does not have the same direct custody.
8 | Both forms require legal justification. Neither is a finding of guilt, and judicial custody does not mean a conviction.

# 9 · RIGHTS IN CUSTODY
8 | Custody does not remove basic rights. Section Forty Seven requires the grounds of arrest to be communicated forthwith.
6 | Section Forty Eight requires that a nominated relative or friend be informed.

8 | Sections Fifty Three and Fifty Six provide for medical examination, and for the arrested person's health and safety.
8 | And bail can be sought before the maximum custody periods expire: the court need not use every available day.
