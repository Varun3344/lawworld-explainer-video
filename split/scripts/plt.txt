# 1 · REJECTED OR RETURNED?
10 | You file a civil suit. The judge says this plaint cannot proceed. Is it rejected, or returned? Each leads to a different procedure, and a different remedy.
8 | The answer lies in Order Seven of the Code of Civil Procedure, or C P C, and four leading Supreme Court judgments.
# 2 · TWO RULES, TWO QUESTIONS
10 | Two rules, two questions. Rule Ten deals with return: is this the proper court? It commonly applies when territorial or pecuniary jurisdiction is missing.
8 | Rule Eleven deals with rejection: does a statutory ground stop the plaint from proceeding? A weak case alone is not enough.
# 3 · SIX GROUNDS FOR REJECTION
10 | Which grounds count? Rule Eleven lists six. First, the plaint discloses no cause of action. Second, the relief is undervalued, and not corrected in the time fixed.
10 | Third, the plaint is insufficiently stamped, and the stamp paper is not supplied in time. Fourth, the suit appears, from the statement in the plaint, to be barred by law.
10 | Fifth, the plaint is not filed in duplicate. Sixth, the plaintiff fails to comply with Rule Nine. For valuation and stamp defects, the plaintiff first gets time to correct them.
# 4 · A MEANINGFUL READING
10 | How closely must a court read a plaint? In T Arivandandam versus T V Satyapal, the Supreme Court said the plaint must receive a meaningful reading. || How closely must a court read a plaint? In T. Arivandandam v. T.V. Satyapal, the Supreme Court said the plaint must receive a meaningful reading.
8 | Clever drafting cannot manufacture a cause of action where none exists. So the court must look past the drafting to the substance.
10 | Picture a plaint full of impressive allegations, but missing the material facts that create a right to relief. Drafting a plaint? Plead those material facts.
10 | But the court must identify a ground under Rule Eleven. It cannot reject a plaint merely because the case looks difficult. Seeking rejection? Name the ground.

10 | What may the court read? In Dahiben versus Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali, the answer is the plaint as a whole, with the documents it relies on. || What may the court read? In Dahiben v. Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali, the answer is the plaint as a whole, with the documents it relies on.

10 | Practice point: check the pleaded dates and documents carefully. Limitation supports rejection only when the bar is apparent from the plaint.
6 | Acting for the plaintiff? Disputed facts cannot simply be resolved against your client at this stage.
# 6 · HOW RETURN WORKS
6 | The flip side is return. A properly pleaded claim may still be filed in the wrong court.
10 | Under Rule Ten, the plaint is returned for presentation to the court where the suit should have been instituted. Return is about the forum, not the merits.
8 | The judge endorses the plaint with the dates of presentation and return, the presenter's name, and brief reasons for returning it.
6 | Where its conditions apply, Rule Ten A can fix an appearance date in the proper court. || Where its conditions apply, Rule 10A can fix an appearance date in the proper court.
# 7 · IN THE PROPER COURT
10 | What happens in the proper court? In O N G C versus Modern Construction, the Supreme Court said the returned plaint is treated as a fresh institution. || What happens in the proper court? In ONGC v. Modern Construction, the Supreme Court said the returned plaint is treated as a fresh institution.
6 | The trial proceeds afresh. It does not continue the earlier suit, so limitation matters.
10 | Section Fourteen of the Limitation Act may exclude the time spent pursuing the case in a court without jurisdiction. But that protection must be established.
6 | Return does not preserve the original filing date, so never assume it protects your client.
# 8 · RETURN ≠ TRANSFER
8 | Is a return just a transfer? No. The three judge bench in E X L Careers versus Frankfinn Aviation Services confirmed it. || Is a return just a transfer? No. The three-judge bench in EXL Careers v. Frankfinn Aviation Services confirmed it.
6 | After a return under Rules Ten and Ten A, the proceedings must begin afresh. || After a return under Rules 10 and 10A, the proceedings must begin afresh.
8 | Rule Ten A simplifies appearance and service. It does not turn a return into a transfer that keeps the earlier trial stage. || Rule 10A simplifies appearance and service. It does not turn a return into a transfer that keeps the earlier trial stage.
10 | One qualification. In E X L itself, the Supreme Court used its constitutional powers on exceptional facts, and left the High Court's order undisturbed.
6 | That relief does not change the general rule. Cite E X L for the rule, not for its result.
# 9 · DIFFERENT REMEDIES
10 | Rejected? That rejection is deemed a decree under Section Two, sub section Two. So it ordinarily attracts a first appeal under Section Ninety Six.
10 | An order returning a plaint is appealable under Order Forty Three, Rule One, clause A, except where the Rule Ten A procedure was followed. || An order returning a plaint is appealable under Order XLIII Rule 1(a), except where the Rule 10A procedure was followed.
6 | Rejection does not, by itself, bar a fresh plaint on the same cause of action. Rule Thirteen says so.
8 | But a fresh plaint must still overcome limitation and the legal defect. Refiling a barred claim does not remove the bar.
# 10 · APPLY THE DISTINCTION
10 | Test yourself with two plaints. The first pleads a recoverable debt, but the chosen court lacks territorial jurisdiction. Think return, under Rule Ten.
8 | The second plaint, read with its own documents, discloses no cause of action. Think rejection, under Rule Eleven.
10 | Before drafting or opposing either application, check these four things: the forum, the material facts, the documents relied on, and limitation.
10 | Return sends the plaint to the proper court. Rejection follows a specified statutory ground. Get it right: that distinction decides the next step in the entire case.
