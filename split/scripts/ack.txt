// Facts used (para): HC order 04.11.2024 in C.R.P. 66/2020 allowed the defendant's revision and rejected the plaint as barred by
// limitation (2, 3.5). Suit filed in 2014 under Order XXXVII CPC for money due under a civil works contract dated 10.02.2000 with the
// defendant society, for construction work on a multi-storey building in Mayur Vihar, Delhi; running bills raised between 2000 and
// 2003; claim Rs 11,16,000 + interest Rs 11,04,840 at 9% p.a. (3.1). The defendant's Order VII Rule 11 application: barred by
// limitation, no cause of action, not maintainable (3.2). Trial Court, 06.01.2020: application rejected; the outstanding amounts were
// reflected in the society's balance sheets "for the years 2004, 2005 till 2013, which amounts to acknowledgment of debt consequently
// giving rise to a fresh period of limitation"; maintainability and genuineness of bills need evidence (3.3). Revision: suit of 2014
// for transactions of 2000 and 2003 time-barred (3.4). HC: bank statements no acknowledgment beyond 2005 (3.5); the balance sheet as on
// 31.03.2013 reflected Rs 21,79,465 under sundry creditors pertaining to 2004-05 "but it did not in any way indicate that the amount was
// due and payable to the original plaintiff" (8). The appeal is by the original plaintiff's legal heir (3.6). The limited question:
// whether, given the balance-sheet entries up to 2013, the suit could be rejected at threshold as time-barred (5). Bills of 2000-2003
// not in dispute; the society had issued cheques to him, which were dishonoured; he relies on balance-sheet entries FY 2004-05 to
// FY 2012-13 (6), especially the entry dated 31.03.2013 (7). Section 18 Limitation Act: a written acknowledgment of a subsisting
// liability gives rise to a fresh period of limitation (9). Bishal Jaiswal, 2021 INSC 254 (three judges): balance-sheet entries may be
// acknowledgments, read with the balance sheet as a whole, notes and auditor's report (10); Axis Bank v. Naren Sheth, 2023 INSC 820
// (11). The 2004-05 bank reconciliation statements record three cheques issued in favour of "Wazir Hussain Contractor" (12); later
// balance sheets carried forward Rs 21,79,134 under "Balances pertaining to 2004-05" (13).
// Held: Section 18 needs no naming of the creditor and no fixed format, so long as it acknowledges a subsisting liability, read with
// the surrounding circumstances; here the liability was in the society's own balance sheets, carried forward to 2013, "renewing the
// limitation period every year" (14). Not conclusive that the whole amount is owed: "These are matters for trial"; at the Order VII
// Rule 11 stage the court "ought not to have gone into the depths of evidence and conclusively determined the non-existence of
// liability", only whether the plaint disclosed a prima facie case on the material (15). The plea that a time-barred debt cannot be
// revived indefinitely, especially without a condonation application, "cannot be sustained": since the entries are an acknowledgment
// under Section 18, "a fresh period of limitation would automatically run from each subsequent date of acknowledgment", so "there was
// no requirement for an application seeking condonation of delay" (16). HC order set aside; "The suit shall stand revived on the file
// of the Trial Court. All questions on the merits of the claim shall remain open." (17); appeal allowed (18).
