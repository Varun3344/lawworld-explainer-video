//  - s.2(1)(d) (c06): "document" means any matter expressed or described or otherwise recorded upon any substance ... "and includes
//    electronic and digital records"; Illustration (vi): "An electronic record on emails, server logs, documents on computers, laptop or
//    smartphone, messages, websites, locational evidence and voice mail messages stored on digital devices are documents" (panel only).

//    Court" (panel only).
//  - s.61 (c07): "Nothing in this Adhiniyam shall apply to deny the admissibility of an electronic or digital record in the evidence on
//    the ground that it is an electronic or digital record and such record shall, subject to section 63, have the same legal effect,
//    validity and enforceability as other document." s.62 (panel): "The contents of electronic records may be proved in accordance with
//    the provisions of section 63." (= IEA s.65A).
//  - s.57 (c08-c10): "Primary evidence means the document itself produced for the inspection of the Court." Explanations 4-7 are NEW
//    (IEA s.62 had 1-3 only): Expl. 4 a record created or stored "simultaneously or sequentially in multiple files, each such file is
//    primary evidence"; Expl. 5 "Where an electronic or digital record is produced from proper custody, such electronic and digital
//    record is primary evidence unless it is disputed"; Expl. 6 a video recording simultaneously stored in electronic form and
//    transmitted, broadcast or transferred - "each of the stored recordings is primary evidence" (panel only); Expl. 7 stored in multiple
//    storage spaces in a computer resource - "each such automated storage, including temporary files, is primary evidence". c09 merges
//    Expl. 4 and 7; c10 is Expl. 5.
//  - s.63(1) (c11, c12): any information contained in an electronic record "printed on paper, stored, recorded or copied in optical or
//    magnetic media or semiconductor memory which is produced by a computer or any communication device" ... ("the computer output")
//    "shall be deemed to be also a document, if the conditions mentioned in this section are satisfied" and "shall be admissible in any
//    proceedings, without further proof or production of the original". A copy made from the DVR onto a pen drive = information copied
//    in semiconductor memory = computer output (c11).
//  - s.63(2)(a)-(d) (c13, c14): (a) output produced while the device was used regularly to create, store or process information for an
//    activity regularly carried on "by the person having lawful control over the use" of it; (b) information of that kind "regularly fed"
//    in the ordinary course; (c) the device "operating properly" or, if not, not such as to affect the record or the accuracy of its
//    contents; (d) the information "reproduces or is derived from" information so fed. s.63(3) (standalone, computer system, network,
//    computer resource or intermediary = a single device): panel only.
//  - s.63(4) (c15-c17): "a certificate doing any of the following things shall be submitted along with the electronic record at each
//    instance where it is being submitted for admission" (these words are new vs s.65B(4)); (a) identifies the record and describes "the
//    manner in which it was produced"; (b) particulars of the device; (c) deals with the s.63(2) conditions, "purporting to be signed by a
//    person in charge of the computer or communication device or the management of the relevant activities (whichever is appropriate) and
//    an expert"; "sufficient for a matter to be stated to the best of the knowledge and belief of the person stating it in the
//    certificate specified in the Schedule". Old IEA s.65B(4)(c) (as quoted in Anvar): signed by "a person occupying a responsible
//    official position in relation to the operation of the relevant device or the management of the relevant activities" - NO expert,
//    hence c16 "That is new".
//  - THE SCHEDULE "[See section 63(4)(c)] Certificate" (c18-c21): Part A "(To be filled by the Party)": source tick-boxes (Computer /
//    Storage Media, DVR, Mobile, Flash Drive, CD/DVD, Server, Cloud, Other), make & model, colour, serial number, IMEI/UIN/UID/MAC/Cloud
//    ID; the source "was under the lawful control for regularly creating, storing or processing information", the device "was working
//    properly", the information "regularly fed ... during the ordinary course of business"; any malfunction "has not affected the
//    electronic/digital record or its accuracy"; the source is "Owned / Maintained / Managed / Operated by me (select as applicable)";

//    (Legally acceptable standard) (Hash report to be enclosed with the certificate)"; name, signature, date, time (IST), place.
//    Part B "(To be filled by the Expert)": the same source / device particulars and the same hash statement and algorithm; name,
//    designation and signature. c19 says "own, manage or operate" (Maintained left to the panel).
//  - s.39 (c25 footer): (1) experts in "foreign law, science or art, or any other field"; (2) for information in a computer resource or

# 2 · ELECTRONIC RECORDS ARE DOCUMENTS
8 | Are these documents? Yes. Under Section Two, sub section One, clause D, a document includes electronic and digital records.
8 | Section Sixty One bars refusing a record only because it is electronic. But Section Sixty Three still applies.
# 3 · PRIMARY OR SECONDARY?
8 | Primary evidence is the document itself. Section Fifty Seven now adds explanations for electronic records.
8 | Where a record sits in multiple files or storage spaces, even temporary ones, each is primary evidence.
6 | And so is a record produced from proper custody, unless it is disputed.
8 | But Nisha's pen drive holds a copy from the D V R. It is a computer output, governed by Section Sixty Three. || But Nisha's pen drive holds a copy from the DVR. It is a computer output, governed by Section 63.
# 4 · SECTION 63: THE CONDITIONS
8 | Sub section One deems a computer output a document, admissible without the original, if the conditions are met.
10 | Sub section Two lists them. The device was regularly used by the person in lawful control, and such information was regularly fed in.
8 | It worked properly, or a fault did not affect the record. And the record reproduces, or derives from, what was fed in.
# 5 · THE CERTIFICATE AND ITS SCHEDULE
10 | Sub section Four requires a certificate. It identifies the record, explains how it was produced, describes the device, and addresses those conditions.
8 | It is signed by a person in charge of the device or relevant activities, and also by an expert. That is new.
8 | It must accompany the record at each submission for admission, and may be stated to the best of knowledge and belief.
8 | The Schedule gives the form: Part A for the party, and Part B for the expert. Both identify the source device.
8 | In Part A, the party affirms lawful control, regular use and proper working, and whether they own, manage or operate it.
6 | For the bakery footage, Nisha, who runs the D V R, could sign Part A, and an expert Part B. || For the bakery footage, Nisha, who runs the DVR, could sign Part A, and an expert Part B.
# 6 · HASH VALUE: A DIGITAL FINGERPRINT
8 | Both parts also state the record's hash value, and the algorithm used to calculate it. A hash report is enclosed.
8 | A hash value is a short code calculated from a file's contents. Change even one character, and the hash value changes.
8 | So if the copy in court gives the same hash value, the data has not changed since the certificate recorded it.
10 | In May twenty twenty six, the Supreme Court declined a challenge to this rule. It called a hash value an electronic fingerprint, ensuring integrity. || In May 2026, the Supreme Court declined a challenge to this rule. It called a hash value an electronic fingerprint, ensuring integrity.
10 | It also observed that, besides notified examiners, a person the court finds skilled in cyber forensics may sign Part B, but kept the question open.
# 7 · WHAT THE SUPREME COURT HELD
10 | In twenty fourteen, a three judge bench of the Supreme Court held the old Section Sixty Five B certificate mandatory for secondary electronic evidence. || In 2014, a three-judge bench of the Supreme Court held the old Section 65B certificate mandatory for secondary electronic evidence.
8 | Electronic records are more open to tampering, so the certificate safeguards their source and authenticity.
8 | In twenty twenty, another three judge bench called it a condition precedent. Oral evidence cannot replace it. || In 2020, another three-judge bench called it a condition precedent. Oral evidence cannot replace it.
10 | It held a certificate unnecessary when the original is produced, as when a phone's owner enters the witness box and proves the device is his.
# 8 · WHEN THE CERTIFICATE IS MISSING
8 | What if the person who must sign refuses? The party can apply to the court, which can summon that person to produce it.
8 | In criminal trials, electronic evidence must reach the accused before the trial begins, so a defence can be prepared.
8 | Until the hearing ends, the judge may still allow a certificate later, if the accused is not seriously prejudiced.
# 9 · SCREENSHOTS, EMAILS AND CALL RECORDS
8 | A WhatsApp screenshot or email printout is a computer output too. Without a proper certificate, it risks exclusion.
10 | Call records come from the service provider's system, so its responsible officer should certify them. Providers keep them only for a limited time.
8 | Common mistakes? A screenshot with no certificate, a signer with no responsibility for the device, or no expert part.

10 | Under Section One Seventy, trials, investigations and other proceedings pending when the Adhiniyam began continue under the old Evidence Act.
