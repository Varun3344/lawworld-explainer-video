# 1 · CAN AN FIR BE QUASHED?
8 | Can a High Court end a criminal case before trial? Yes, but only rarely, and never just because the accused calls it false.
8 | Hi, I'm Ravishankar from LawWorld. When will the High Court quash an F I R or a criminal case, and when will it refuse?
10 | The power sits in Section Five Twenty Eight of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, or B N S S. It succeeds Section Four Eighty Two of the old Code.
# 2 · WHAT SECTION 528 SAYS
10 | Nothing in the Sanhita limits the High Court's inherent powers to give effect to orders under it, prevent abuse of process, or secure the ends of justice.
6 | It creates no new power. It preserves a power that belongs to the High Court alone.
# 3 · WHAT CAN BE QUASHED
8 | What can be quashed? An F I R and its investigation, a charge sheet, a summoning order, or the proceedings themselves.
8 | A charge sheet does not close the door. Anand Kumar Mohatta, under the old Code, says abuse of process may then be aggravated.
6 | Pepsi Foods, also under the old Code, calls summoning an accused a serious matter.
# 4 · THE BHAJAN LAL CATEGORIES
10 | The leading case is State of Haryana versus Bhajan Lal, under the old Code and Article Two Twenty Six. It gives seven illustrative categories.
6 | One: the allegations, even taken at face value and accepted in full, make out no offence.
10 | Two: no cognizable offence is disclosed to justify police investigation. Three: the uncontroverted allegations and evidence disclose no offence.
8 | Four: only a non cognizable offence is disclosed, which police cannot investigate without a Magistrate's order.
8 | Five: the allegations are so absurd and inherently improbable that no prudent person could find sufficient ground to proceed.
6 | Six: an express legal bar to the case, or a specific provision giving effective redress.
8 | Seven: the case is manifestly mala fide, or maliciously filed to wreak vengeance out of a private, personal grudge.
8 | The Court stressed that this list is not exhaustive, and that the power must be used very sparingly, with circumspection.
# 5 · WHAT THE HIGH COURT WILL NOT DO
8 | The High Court does not hold a mini trial, or test whether the allegations are true. Disputed defences are left for trial. || The High Court does not hold a mini-trial, or test whether the allegations are true. Disputed defences are left for trial.
8 | Neeharika Infrastructure, under the old Code, says courts should not thwart investigation. Quashing is the exception, not the rule.
8 | Interim protection must not be routine. An interim stay needs brief reasons, and a no coercive steps order must say what it means. || Interim protection must not be routine. An interim stay needs brief reasons, and a “no coercive steps” order must say what it means.
# 6 · A BUSINESS DISPUTE WITH A CRIMINAL COLOUR
8 | Here is a hypothetical. Harish pays Kavya an advance for furniture. Delivery runs late, and he withholds the balance.
8 | Instead of suing, Harish files an F I R alleging cheating and forgery. Kavya asks the High Court to quash it.
8 | Paramjeet Batra, under the old Code, tells the High Court to see whether a civil dispute is given a cloak of criminal offence.
8 | The Supreme Court has also held that a mere breach of contract is not cheating, without dishonest intention at the start.
8 | But a civil dispute can involve a real offence. If the F I R gives specific facts of forgery, the case may proceed.
# 7 · SETTLEMENT IN NON-COMPOUNDABLE CASES
8 | Now suppose they settle. Kavya refunds part of the advance, and Harish files an affidavit supporting quashing.
8 | Section Three Fifty Nine lists the offences that can be compounded, some only with the court's permission. Forgery is not among them.
8 | Gian Singh, under the old Code, holds that the High Court may still quash on settlement, using a power distinct from compounding.
10 | Cases with an overwhelmingly civil flavour, like commercial, matrimonial or family disputes, may be quashed once the parties resolve their entire dispute.
10 | Heinous offences like murder, rape and dacoity affect society, so settlement alone cannot end them, nor Prevention of Corruption Act cases.
10 | Narinder Singh and Parbatbhai Aahir, also under the old Code, add that timing matters, and economic offences affecting the State may not be quashed.
# 8 · OTHER ROUTES TO THE HIGH COURT
8 | Another route is revision under Sections Four Thirty Eight to Four Forty Two, but not against interlocutory orders.

8 | The Supreme Court has also held, under the old Code, that the revision remedy does not, by itself, bar the inherent power.
8 | Quashing petitions are also filed under Article Two Twenty Six. Bhajan Lal's categories cover both powers.
# 9 · PREPARING THE PETITION
8 | In practice, attach the F I R or charge sheet under challenge, and show which category applies from the prosecution's own papers.
10 | In a settlement case, file the complainant's affidavit. The State and the complainant are heard, and the court must find the settlement real and complete.
8 | If the petition fails, the case simply continues. That is not a finding of guilt; the trial court still weighs the evidence.
# 10 · REMEMBER THE TEST
8 | Remember: Section Five Twenty Eight exists to prevent abuse of process. It is used sparingly, and the facts decide.
6 | For Kavya, everything turns on what the F I R alleges, and what was truly settled.
