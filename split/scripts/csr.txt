# 1 · WHAT FOLLOWS THE INVESTIGATION
10 | Does a charge sheet mean the accused is guilty? Does a closure report mean the case is over for good? Both ideas are wrong. Here is what really happens.
10 | Hi, I'm Ravishankar from LawWorld. The F I R has been registered, and the police have finished investigating. So what exactly happens next?
8 | Police may file a charge sheet that recommends prosecution, or a closure report recommending that the case should not proceed.
10 | Under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, or B N S S, the police report informs the court. It does not replace the court's decision.
# 2 · WHAT A CHARGE SHEET CONTAINS
10 | Section One Ninety Three, sub section Three, requires the police report to be sent to the Magistrate when the investigation is completed.
8 | A charge sheet is the report that supports prosecution. It identifies the parties, the allegations and the witnesses.
8 | It states whether an offence appears to have been committed, and by whom. Documents relied on, and witness statements, go with it.
10 | This is the police assessment of the material. Filing a charge sheet is different from the court framing charges, and neither amounts to conviction.
# 3 · EXAMPLE: A THEFT INVESTIGATION
10 | Let's take an example. Ravi reports that his laptop has been stolen. Police examine the C C T V footage, and they speak to the witnesses.
10 | They recover a laptop they believe links Arjun to the theft. If the material supports prosecution, they submit a charge sheet explaining that conclusion.
8 | Arjun can challenge the allegation and the evidence. Police have recommended proceeding, but the court still plays its own role.
# 4 · WHEN POLICE RECOMMEND CLOSURE
8 | Now suppose the footage is unclear, the laptop is not recovered, and there is not enough material to proceed against anyone.
8 | Police may then file a closure report explaining their findings. In other cases, they may conclude that no offence occurred.
8 | Reasons matter. An untraced offender, or insufficient evidence, does not automatically mean Ravi's complaint was false.
10 | The report is a recommendation for judicial consideration. It is not an acquittal after trial, nor an automatic end to every further step.
# 5 · THE COURT AND THE INFORMANT
10 | The Magistrate is not bound by the police conclusion. It may accept closure and drop proceedings, or disagree and take cognizance on sufficient material.
8 | Or it may direct further investigation. But it cannot compel police to take a particular view on prosecution.
8 | Before accepting closure and dropping proceedings, the court must give the informant notice, and an opportunity to be heard.
8 | The Supreme Court set out these three options and this notice safeguard in Bhagwant Singh, under the old Code of Criminal Procedure.
10 | Ravi may file objections, commonly called a protest petition. For example, he might point to an important witness the police overlooked.
10 | The court chooses the lawful course. Objections do not guarantee prosecution, and a protest treated as a complaint follows the complaint procedure.
# 6 · INVESTIGATION TIMELINES ARE DIFFERENT
10 | Is every charge sheet due within ninety days? No. Section One Ninety Three, sub section One, requires investigation without unnecessary delay.
8 | Sub section Two: two months, only for listed sexual and Pocso offences, counted from recording the information. || Sub-section (2): two months, only for listed sexual and POCSO offences, counted from recording the information.
8 | Sub section Three separately requires a progress update to the informant or victim within ninety days.
6 | The action taken must also be communicated to the original informant.
8 | That update is no deadline for finishing every investigation. Always identify the offence, and its own timeline.
# 7 · 60 OR 90 DAYS AND DEFAULT BAIL
10 | The sixty or ninety day limits in Section One Eighty Seven, sub section Three, depend on the offence. They concern investigation detention and default bail. || The 60- or 90-day limits in Section 187(3) depend on the offence. They concern investigation detention and default bail.
10 | If the required report is not filed in time, an accused in custody may claim default bail. But the claim must be timely, and bail furnished.
10 | Expiry does not erase the F I R, or bar a later charge sheet. And it releases no one who has not claimed bail and met the legal conditions.
# 8 · COPIES FOR THE DEFENCE AND VICTIM
10 | In cases instituted on a police report, Section Two Thirty requires the Magistrate to give the accused specified papers free, without delay.
8 | That must happen within fourteen days from the accused's production or appearance. The clock does not run from the date of filing.
10 | A victim represented by an advocate also gets them. They include the police report, the F I R, and relevant statements and documents.
6 | Exclusions apply, bulky papers may be inspected instead, and electronic supply counts.
# 9 · NEW EVIDENCE AND A SUPPLEMENTARY REPORT
10 | A police report does not bar further investigation. Under Section One Ninety Three, sub section Nine, new evidence can lead to a supplementary report.
10 | During trial, further investigation needs the trial court's permission, and must be completed within ninety days, extendable with its permission.
8 | For Ravi, a newly discovered recording could justify examining evidence that was unavailable earlier.
6 | A further report adds to the earlier record. It does not automatically replace it.
# 10 · REMEMBER THE DIFFERENCE
8 | A charge sheet recommends prosecution. A closure report recommends closure. Either way, the court decides the next legal step.
10 | The informant has a right to be heard before closure is accepted. Check each deadline separately, get the relevant papers, and respond to notices promptly.
