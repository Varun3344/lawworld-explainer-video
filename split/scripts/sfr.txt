// Facts used (para): appeals by the plaintiff against the decree and common order of the VI Additional District Judge, Markapur, of
// 15.02.2012, allowing three I.A.s for rejection of the plaint under Order VII Rule 11 CPC in O.S. No. 43 of 2011, a suit for specific
// performance of an agreement of sale dated 06.01.2010 (1). Plaint: D1 to D4 (a mother, two daughters, a son-in-law) own 47.50 acres
// (10); on the night of 05.01.2010 D1, the eldest daughter managing the properties, agreed for herself and D2-D4 to sell for
// Rs.2,38,00,000; Rs.5,00,000 earnest money paid; the four terms written by D1 in her own hand and signed (11); in August 2010 he
// learnt that D1-D4 had executed nine documents (Doc. Nos. 5840-5848/2010, all of 20.08.2010) in favour of D5-D9 (15-16); suit for
// specific performance (17-18). I.A.s by D2-D4, D1 and D5-D9 (19): the paper does not show who executed it or in whose favour, or a
// description of the property (21); white paper, liable to stamp duty and penalty (22); only terms to enter into an agreement of sale
// on 18.01.2010, an agreement to enter into an agreement is not enforceable (23); no written authorisation to D1 (25). Plaintiff: D1
// did not deny her handwriting or receiving Rs.5,00,000; the trial court rejected the plaint on the ground that an agreement to enter
// into another agreement is not enforceable, no authority, no names of vendors and vendees, no schedule (4); it nipped the plaint at
// the threshold (5). Held: the Court looks only at the averments of the plaint and its documents, no mini-trial (30); the agreement's
// recitals: 47.50 acres in Narasimhapuram and Markapuram, total Rs.2,38,00,000, 25% (Rs.60,00,000) with Rs.5,00,000 advance paid on
// 06.01.2010, balance Rs.1,78,00,000 within four months and registration (34, 70); "all the essential ingredients of an agreement";
// signatures of D1, the plaintiff and two witnesses; the defendants did not dispute her handwriting, the Rs.5,00,000 advance or her
// signature (35, 60, 67); identification of the property not disputed (44-45); parties and authorisation are for trial (51, 57, 63);
// "These disputed points on authorization and allied aspects shall be adjudicated only after trial" (66); an agreement to enter into
// an agreement is not enforceable (69), but the defendants could not show any missing material term; "The Court also prima facie
// finds that the agreement contains all essential terms", enforceable subject to proof, to be decided after trial (73); the trial
// court "mainly relied on the principle that an agreement to execute another agreement is not enforceable without referring to the
// contents of the agreement" (75); rejection is "a drastic power", Salim D. Agboatwala (77); the dispute pending about 16 years
// without trial (85); "the impugned Order of the Trial Court in rejecting the plaint is not sustainable" (90); stamp duty, T.P. Act,
// Registration Act - no grounds for rejection, may be raised at the appropriate stage (98). RESULT: the three appeals allowed with
// costs; no view on the merits, the trial court to proceed on the merits uninfluenced (101); the plaintiff to deposit the balance
// sale consideration by way of fixed deposits within 10 days, subject to the result; on failure the defendants may seek leave to
// alienate under S. 52 T.P. Act (102); the suit to be disposed of preferably within 6 months of receipt of the judgment (103).

# 1 · THROWN OUT BEFORE TRIAL?
10 | You pay an advance on a handwritten deal for farmland, and the sellers then sell it to others. Can a court throw out your suit before any trial even begins?
10 | In Andhra Pradesh, a trial court did just that, mainly calling the deal a mere agreement to agree. The buyer appealed to the High Court. Was the deal incomplete?
