//    s.173: it rests on practice and Satvinder Kaur (old Code), hence "generally transferred" (c09). No e-Zero-FIR, portal, helpline

# 1 · WHERE CAN YOU REPORT A CRIME?
8 | Your phone is stolen in another city. Must you go back there to report it? Under the B N S S, you need not.
10 | Hi, I'm Ravishankar from LawWorld. Where, and how, can an F I R be lodged under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita? What if it is not recorded?
8 | Here is a hypothetical. Meera, a teacher from Mysuru, has her phone stolen at a bus stand in Hubballi.
# 2 · WHAT AN FIR IS
10 | Section One Seventy Three, sub section One, deals with information about a cognizable offence. Once recorded, it is commonly called the F I R.
8 | It may be given in writing, orally, or electronically, to the officer in charge of a police station.
6 | Oral information is reduced to writing, read over to the informant, and signed.
# 3 · ZERO FIR: REPORT ANYWHERE
8 | Notice the words: irrespective of the area where the offence is committed. That phrase is the basis of the Zero F I R.
8 | Assume Meera's information discloses a cognizable offence. She may report it at a police station in Mysuru.
8 | It is recorded there, and the case is then generally transferred to the police station that has jurisdiction.
10 | Under the old Code of Criminal Procedure, the Supreme Court held in Satvinder Kaur that police cannot refuse an F I R only for lack of jurisdiction.
# 4 · E-FIR: ELECTRONIC INFORMATION
10 | Now suppose Meera reports electronically, from a laptop. That is the E F I R route; the Sanhita calls it electronic communication. || Now suppose Meera reports electronically, from a laptop. That is the e-FIR route; the Sanhita calls it electronic communication.
6 | Under clause Two, it is taken on record once the person giving it signs it within three days. || Under clause (ii), it is taken on record once the person giving it signs it within three days.
# 5 · SAFEGUARDS AND A FREE COPY
10 | When a woman reports specified offences against her, such as sexual offences or an acid attack, a woman officer must record it.
10 | If the victim of these offences is disabled, it is recorded at home or a chosen place, with an interpreter or special educator, and videographed.
8 | Under sub section Two, the informant or the victim gets a free copy of the information as recorded, forthwith.
# 6 · PRELIMINARY ENQUIRY
10 | Under the old Code, the Constitution Bench in Lalita Kumari held registration mandatory when the information discloses a cognizable offence.
10 | A preliminary inquiry was permitted only in limited cases, and only to check whether a cognizable offence was disclosed.
10 | Sub section Three is new. For offences punishable with three years or more but less than seven years, police may hold a preliminary enquiry.
10 | It needs prior permission from a Deputy Superintendent of Police or above, and must check, within fourteen days, whether a prima facie case exists.
8 | The Supreme Court has called this an exception, allowing enquiry even when a cognizable offence is disclosed.
10 | Outside that exception, registration remains mandatory. And if the enquiry finds no prima facie case, the informant should be told immediately.
# 7 · WHEN INFORMATION IS NOT RECORDED
8 | Now suppose the information is not recorded. Section One Seventy Three, sub section Four, gives Meera a remedy.
8 | She may send the substance of her information, in writing and by post, to the Superintendent of Police concerned.
10 | If satisfied that it discloses a cognizable offence, the Superintendent must investigate, or direct a subordinate officer to investigate.
8 | Failing that, she may apply to the Magistrate. Section One Seventy Five, sub section Three, requires an affidavit in support.
10 | The Magistrate may make any necessary inquiry, and must consider the police officer's submissions, before ordering investigation.
10 | The Supreme Court has said these conditions codify earlier judicial safeguards, like Priyanka Srivastava's affidavit rule under the old Code.
# 8 · NON-COGNIZABLE INFORMATION
8 | What if the information discloses only a non cognizable offence? Then Section One Seventy Four applies instead.
6 | The officer records its substance in a book, and refers the informant to the Magistrate.
10 | Police need a Magistrate's order to investigate it. But where at least one offence is cognizable, the whole case is treated as cognizable.
# 9 · WHAT AN FIR IS NOT
6 | Registration is not arrest, and an F I R is not proof of guilt; that is for the court.
8 | Nor is it substantive evidence. It can generally be used only to corroborate or contradict its maker.
8 | Under the old Code, the Supreme Court also held that an F I R is not an encyclopaedia of every fact and detail.
10 | Report promptly. Delay is not fatal by itself, but courts look for a satisfactory explanation, because delay can invite embellishment.
10 | Knowingly giving false information may be an offence, for example under Sections Two Seventeen and Two Forty Eight of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita.
