// Facts used: the landlords' eviction petition under s.14(1)(e) read with s.25-B DRC Act, 1958 for Shop No. 1219/07, Ground Floor,
// Katra Sat Narayan, Chandni Chowk (para 2), on bona fide requirement (19); summons served 23.09.2025, leave to defend filed 06.10.2025
// (3); on 05.10.2026, with leave to defend listed for arguments, the tenants moved a s.151 CPC application to place subsequent events and
// documents on record (4); the material: the landlords' eviction petitions against occupants of other shops in the same premises,
// withdrawals / disposals, and photographs of "SHOP AVAILABLE FOR RENT" banners (8-10, 18); ARC-02 (Central), Tis Hazari dismissed it
// on 06.10.2026 (1, 5); petition under Art. 227 (6); tenants: the material negates / questions the bona fide requirement (8, 10).
// Held: the other petitions were disclosed in para 31 of the eviction petition and para 15 of the reply, and are readily accessible on
// the e-courts portal; the explanations are "vague and not maintainable" (18-19); "not one shred of proof" that the landlords intend to
// or have let out the other shops (20); even if re-let, s.19 DRC Act protects evicted tenants (21, 31); Gaya Prasad v. Pradeep
// Srivastava (2001) 2 SCC 604: the need must be "completely eclipsed" by subsequent events; these fail that threshold (23, 25); the
// application is "a dilatory tactic" (26); s.151 cannot be invoked to give successive opportunities after leave to defend is filed, nor
// to bypass the s.25-B summary procedure (27-28, 31); plausible view, no perversity under Art. 227 (30); petition dismissed (33).
